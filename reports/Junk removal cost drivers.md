# Price the trip, not just the yards

Independent US junk haulers make or lose money on three things more than on cubic yards. The first is the fixed cost of sending a two-person crew and truck out, through the dump and back. The second is crew hours, which stairs, long carries and dump lines can double. The third is disposal, billed by weight at rates from **$34 a ton in Salt Lake County to $243 a ton in King County, WA**, plus per-item fees for mattresses, refrigerant appliances, tires and TVs. HaulCalc's skeleton (a volume price curve, add-on fees, a weight overage and a cost-based profit check) matches how coaches, franchisors and pricing tools describe the job, so it needs re-tuning, not a rebuild. The most important fix is the default curve, which climbs a flat $600 per load from 1/8 to 3/4 and then jumps; that leaves 1/8–3/4 loads roughly 10–30% below the ratios in published independent price lists, and by HaulCalc's own cost model a 1/8 load breaks even and a single item loses money while a full load clears 69%. Next, the couch, appliance and electronics fees are full single-item prices added on top of a volume price that already pays for the item's space, so a lone refrigerator quotes at about **$285 against a market price near $110–$130**; these fees should shrink to disposal surcharges of $15–$40 or go away, with hot tubs, pianos and safes priced on their own lines. After that, the photo range should lean upward for items nobody photographed and learn from finished jobs; dense loads need a payload cap (a 7x14 trailer carries only about 4.5–5 cubic yards of broken concrete) and an overage tied to local dump rates; and the cost basis needs dump time, payroll taxes, card fees and $75 rather than $25 of overhead per job, without which it overstates margins by roughly 20–35 points on mid-size and large loads, so the 50% target margin should fall to about 30% once those costs are in. Treat every figure here with care: researchers worked from search-engine excerpts because page loads were blocked, Reddit, YouTube and podcasts were essentially unreadable, and only the dump-fee, fuel, wage and weight-per-yard figures are official, while the price-curve shape rests on about a dozen operator price lists and the margin and photo-accuracy figures are mostly coach or vendor claims.

## A crew's round trip, not the cubic yard, sets the cost

The most detailed operator-side breakdown of a junk job comes from Junk Removal Authority (JRA), a coaching firm whose pricing pages date from about 2019–2022. On an **average $350 job, direct costs were about $143.50 (41%)**, covering the two-person crew counted door to door (drive, load and dump time) plus disposal, fuel and card fees. That left about $206 before marketing, or roughly $119 per job after the cost of winning customers in a first year ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-much-money-are-you-making-on-each-junk-removal-job/)). Owners who grew report thinner results. Casey Walsh ran about $4 million a year at an **18% profit margin** ([Sweaty Startup](https://www.sweatystartup.com/blog/how-to-start-a-junk-removal-company-with-casey-walsh)), and Kyle Landwehr says his gross margin fell from about 50% in year one to about 20% by year three after he added management ([UpFlip](https://www.upflip.com/blog/how-to-start-junk-removal-business)). These are self-reported interview figures, but they mark out the range a profit check should expect.

Underneath is a large fixed cost per visit. JRA puts the cost of simply getting a truck to a site at **about $70–$80**, says a minimum near $95 leaves roughly $20 of profit, and tells clients to "weight your increases heavier toward the beginning of your load size scale," because most expenses are incurred showing up ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/); [JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/)). Vendor guides make the same point in hours: a "one-hour" job is often three hours of paid crew time once the drive, the dump line, unloading and the return are counted ([GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services)). No one has measured the dump leg, but it is real. One Kansas transfer station calls 1–2-hour waits "normal" at peak times, gives trucks 15 minutes to unload when it is crowded, and closes its line at 3:45 pm on weekdays ([Leavenworth County](https://www.leavenworthcounty.gov/departments/transfer_station/frequently_asked_questions/index.php)). Most jobs are small, too: Angi's national average is **$241, with most jobs between $133 and $372**, across 44,583 projects ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). That makes the fixed trip a large share of most tickets.

Crew hours, the biggest variable cost, depend more on access than on volume. The College Hunks franchisor tells franchisees that stairs, long carries and no elevator "can double load-out time on the same volume" ([College Hunks Franchise](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/)). A site that trains junk removal owners estimates that a second crew member cuts loading time on a typical home job by 35–45% ([Junk Removal 365](https://junkremoval365.com/hiring-your-first-junk-removal-crew-member/)). Helpers earn about $16–$25 an hour in 2026; the federal Bureau of Labor Statistics median for hand laborers and material movers is $18.10, and ZipRecruiter's junk removal average is $17.52 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/Oes/current/oes537062.htm); [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Junk-Removal-Salary)). Employer payroll tax (7.65%) plus workers' compensation (roughly $4.50–$8.00 per $100 of payroll) raises the true cost to about **$21–$24 an hour** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751); [1800insurance](https://www.1800insurance.com/guides/workers-comp-for-trucking)).

Disposal is the cost that varies most by place. Landfills and transfer stations mostly weigh the truck and charge per ton, with a minimum per visit of usually $10–$47. The national average landfill fee was **$62.28 a ton in 2024 and $63.48 in the 2025 report** from EREF, the waste industry's research foundation ([Waste Advantage](https://wasteadvantagemag.com/eref-report-shows-10-increase-in-u-s-landfill-tipping-fees-largest-increase-since-2022/); [Waste Today](https://www.wastetodaymagazine.com/news/eref-landfill-tipping-fees-usa-2025-report-golf-event-research/)), and regional averages ran from $43 in the Southeast to $84 in the Northeast in 2023 ([EREF](https://erefdn.org/analyzing-municipal-solid-waste-landfill-tipping-fees/)). City transfer stations, where urban haulers actually unload, charge far more. King County, WA charges **$243.38 a ton** in 2026 with a $40.25 minimum ([King County](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/WAKING/bulletins/3f43a3b)), Seattle charges $165 ([Seattle Public Utilities](https://web5.seattle.gov/utilities/your-services/collection-and-disposal/transfer-stations/rates)), Portland's regional system about $158 plus $8.34 per load ([Metro](https://www.oregonmetro.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/rate-and-fee-setting-fact-sheet.pdf)), and Morris County, NJ $113 ([MCMUA](https://mcmua.com/sw_ts_tippingfees.asp)), against $34 in Salt Lake County ([Salt Lake County](https://saltlakecounty.gov/landfill/pricing)). Haulers with a commercial account often pay 25–50% below the posted rate. One Ohio operator pays about $90 a ton against a $130 posted rate, yet disposal is still about 15% of its sales ([Ohio Junk Force](https://ohiojunkforce.com/blog/what-does-junk-removal-actually-cost-and-why/)). Household junk is light; one operator estimates **about 2,300 lb for a full 15-yard trailer** ([Snap Junk Removal](https://snapjunkremoval.com/blog_feed/how-much-does-junk-weigh/)). So dumping a full household load costs roughly $52 in the South and $280 in King County, while the same trailer filled with mixed construction debris weighs about three times as much.

Overhead is dominated by marketing. Google Local Services Ads leads cost about $30–$65 each, or roughly **$45–$110 per booked job** ([Pushleads](https://pushleads.com/junk-removal-marketing/junk-removal-cost-per-lead-2/); [CurbWaste](https://www.curbwaste.com/blog/google-ads-for-junk-removal-and-dumpster-rental)). That is more than insurance, where published averages run about $810–$1,960 a year for general liability and $2,075–$2,381 per vehicle for commercial auto ([The Hartford](https://www.thehartford.com/business-insurance/junk-removal-insurance); [MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/business/junk-removal/cost); [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/construction-contracting-business-insurance/debris-removal/cost)). Spreading a one-truck business's fixed costs and marketing over a realistic 40–60 jobs a month gives roughly **$60–$100 of overhead per job**; at 20 jobs a month it balloons past $200 (this report's arithmetic from those vendor-sourced inputs).

### HaulCalc's own defaults already flag the problem

The table runs HaulCalc's current default settings on a household-junk job 10 miles from base with no stairs. It assumes 200 lb per cubic yard, which matches the app's 3,000-lb included weight for a full 15-cubic-yard (yd³) load.

| Load | Price today | Cost at HaulCalc's defaults | Profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single item (~1 yd³) | $100 | $123 | −$23 | −23% |
| 1/8 | $125 | $123 | $2 | 2% |
| 1/4 | $200 | $123 | $77 | 39% |
| 1/2 | $350 | $152 | $198 | 57% |
| 3/4 | $500 | $196 | $304 | 61% |
| Full | $765 | $240 | $525 | 69% |

Small jobs carry the same $40 dump minimum, half-hour of loading, drive and $25 overhead as a quarter load, but bring in less. This is the "linear load pricing" mistake JRA warns about, visible in HaulCalc's own numbers. Cost-plus mode makes the reverse error: at a 50% target it would quote about **$246 for anything up to a quarter load and $481 for a full load**, roughly double Angi's $75–$125 for a 1/8 load and well under its $600–$800 for a full truck ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm)). The customer price has to come from what the local market pays; the cost model's job is to tell the operator when that price stops covering the trip.

## Six fixes, ranked by how much they change accuracy and profit

HaulCalc's structure matches how practitioners price. JRA recommends a ladder of truck fractions with a set price at every step that restarts for each additional truckload, and the College Hunks franchisor's ideal pricing sheet is a minimum, four load tiers, an hourly rate for labor-only work and a short list of add-on fees ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/); [College Hunks Franchise](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/)). Vendor calculators all apply the same cost ÷ (1 − margin) formula HaulCalc uses ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-price-a-junk-removal-job/)). The changes below are therefore about default values and a handful of missing variables, ordered by how many quotes they touch and how much money each one moves. The curve affects every job, item fees affect many jobs, and the volume range drives everything else. Dense materials are rare but cause the largest single-job losses, the cost basis decides whether the profit check can be trusted, and access, travel and timing are fine-tuning.

### Priority 1: Front-load the price curve and lift the minimum to $99

HaulCalc's default curve rises $75 for each eighth of a load between 1/8 and 3/4, a straight line at **$600 per full load**, and then adds $265 for the last quarter, which works out to $1,060 per load. Practitioners do the opposite. JRA's example ladder puts a quarter load at about 42% of the full price and a half load at about 67% ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/)). Published independent price lists are less steep but point the same way. Across Johnny Mac's in Georgia, Big Rob's in Florida, Clean Green in San Diego, Junk Tug in Arizona, and the Two Men and a Truck locations in Westchester, NY and Lansing, MI, 1/8 loads sit at 16–28% of the full price, quarter loads at 29–36%, half loads at 50–67%, and three-quarter loads at about 75% ([Johnny Mac's](https://www.johnnymacsjunkremoval.com/junk-removal-pricing); [Big Rob's](https://bigrobshauling.com/dumpster-rentals/dump-trailer-vs-junk-removal); [Clean Green](https://cghauling.com/pricing%20%20page.html); [Junk Tug](https://junktug.com/junk-removal-pricing); [Two Men and a Truck Westchester](https://twomenandatruck.com/movers/ny/westchester/junk); [Two Men and a Truck Lansing](https://twomenandatruck.com/movers/mi/lansing/junk)). HaulCalc's defaults are 16%, 26%, 46% and 65%.

| Point on curve | HaulCalc today | % of full | Recommended | % of full | Published benchmarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | $75 at zero volume | 10% | $99, flat up to 1/16 load (~1 yd³) | 13% | Independents $85–$128, $99 common; franchises about $89–$200 |
| 1/8 | $125 | 16% | $150 | 20% | Price lists 16–28%; Angi $75–$125 |
| 1/4 | $200 | 26% | $255 | 33% | Price lists 29–36%; JRA 42% |
| 1/2 | $350 | 46% | $430 | 56% | Price lists 50–67%; JRA 67% |
| 3/4 | $500 | 65% | $595 | 78% | Price lists 75–76% |
| Full | $765 | 100% | $765, set to local market | 100% | Independents $380–$875; franchises $600–$1,100 |

The full-load price itself is sound. $765 for 15 yd³ is about **$51 per cubic yard**, inside the $30–$65 range guides give for household junk ([Service Calculator Hub](https://www.servicecalculatorhub.com/junk-removal-cost-per-cubic-yard-calculator/)), and independents charge from $380 for a 10.6-yd³ load in Arizona to $800–$875 for a 14–15-yd³ dump trailer in Colorado ([Junk Tug](https://junktug.com/junk-removal-pricing); [Haul of the Wild](https://haulofthewildservices.com/pricing/)). It should become the one regional setting the operator adjusts, with the curve following as a percentage. Angi's city averages run from 0.87 times the national figure in Charlotte to 1.53 times in New York ([Angi Charlotte](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost/nc/charlotte); [Angi NYC](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost/ny/new-york)), and the same brand charges $1,249 for a full truck in Westchester against $599 in Lansing.

The $75 minimum sits below every independent minimum found: Johnny Mac's charges $85, DMV Go Junk $99 and Swift $128, and Housecall Pro's guidance is $70–$150 ([Johnny Mac's](https://www.johnnymacsjunkremoval.com/junk-removal-pricing); [DMV Go Junk](https://dmvgojunk.com/cost-calculator.html); [Swift Junk Removal](https://www.swift-junk.com/prices); [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/junk-removal-pricing/)). It is also at or below JRA's (dated) $70–$80 cost of showing up. Holding the minimum flat across the first cubic yard follows operator practice: one operator's booking widget covers the first 2 yd³ with its minimum, and Angi describes a 2-yd³ minimum as common ([Markate operator widget](https://www.markate.com/public/widget/booking/products/d36db15f5aa44faddc35fe0457c81d34:46511:d5e95a3c)). A $125 minimum is defensible where dump fees exceed $100 a ton or the free travel radius is wide.

The current rule for jobs over one load (each full load at the top price, plus the remainder priced on the curve) matches JRA's "pricing simply restarts for every truckload," so keep it. The net effect of the new curve is to raise 1/8 through 3/4 loads by **$25–$95 (about 19–28%)**. That moves them into the middle of the market rather than the top: independents charge $279–$575 for a half load, and franchise estimates mostly run $350–$650.

### Priority 2: Make item fees cover disposal, not charge twice for the space

HaulCalc's item fees ($60 mattress, $75–$150 couch, $40–$140 electronics, $120 appliance, $50 freon) look like what companies charge to remove one item by itself. Those standalone prices are roughly a company's minimum charge plus disposal; they are not meant to be added on top of a volume price that already pays for the item's space. Operators who price by volume add much smaller fees. Swift adds $15 per mattress or tire on top of its tiers ([Swift Junk Removal](https://www.swift-junk.com/prices)), and one operator's booking widget adds $10 per mattress or box spring ([Markate](https://www.markate.com/public/widget/booking/products/d36db15f5aa44faddc35fe0457c81d34:46511:d5e95a3c)). An Indianapolis hauler adds $15–$30 for refrigerant ([Indy Pickup](https://www.indypickup1111.com/paid-services)), and a Cincinnati hauler charged $30 for refrigerant plus $25 for stairs on a second-floor fridge ([Cincy Junk Busters](https://www.cincyjunkbusters.com/blog/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost-for-appliances)). A Charlotte hauler adds $25 per TV and $8 per tire, and nothing extra for freon ([704 Junk](https://704junk.com/)). The effect on single-item jobs is easy to see:

| Single-item job | HaulCalc today | Recommended | Market evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Queen mattress (~1.25 yd³) | ~$170 ($108 volume + $60 fee) | ~$135 ($116 + $20) | Independents $65–$125; LoadUp $85–$99; 1-800-GOT-JUNK average ~$101 |
| Sofa (~2 yd³) | $205–$280 ($130 + $75–$150) | ~$155 (volume only) | LoadUp $84–$134; Move.org $180; guides $75–$200 |
| Refrigerator (~1.5 yd³) | ~$285 ($115 + $120 + $50) | ~$165 ($130 + $35) | LoadUp from $115; Move.org $110; WeCycle $65–$270 |

Sources for the market column: [RJP](https://www.rjphome4.com/pricing), [LoadUp](https://goloadup.com/junk-removal/), [1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/blog/pricing), [Move.org](https://www.move.org/average-junk-removal-cost/), [WeCycle](https://getwecycle.com/blogs/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost).

Surcharges should track what the operator actually pays, and local rules set that. Mattresses are free at Mattress Recycling Council drop-off sites in California, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Oregon, subject to per-site caps ([Bye Bye Mattress](https://byebyemattress.com/drop-it-off-business/)). Elsewhere they cost about $12–$50 each: $40 in Larimer County, CO, and $12–$20 in the Town of Rochester, NY, doubled if the mattress arrives wet ([Larimer County](https://www.larimer.gov/solidwaste/fees); [Town of Rochester](https://www.townofrochester.ny.gov/departments/transfer_station/using_the_transfer_station.php)). In Massachusetts, which bans mattresses from the trash, Amherst charges $40–$80 ([Amherst](https://www.amherstma.gov/157/Recycling---Solid-Waste)). Refrigerant appliances cost about $10–$50 per unit at landfills, such as Spokane County's $30 plus weight ([Spokane County](https://www.spokanecounty.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/5841)), because federal rules require the scrap yard or landfill to verify the refrigerant was recovered ([eCFR 40 CFR 82.155](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-82/subpart-F/section-82.155)). Flat-screen TVs cost $0–$30 to recycle and old tube (CRT) sets $20–$50, or $1–$1.50 per inch ([Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/how-to-dispose-of-crt-tv)). States including Illinois ban covered electronics from landfills for businesses as well as households ([Illinois EPA](https://epa.illinois.gov/topics/waste-management/materials-management/electronics-recycling1/faqs.html)), so TVs cannot be hidden in a mixed load. Washers and dryers without refrigerant carry scrap value instead, with light iron fetching $110–$215 a ton in fall 2026 ([Rockaway Recycling](https://rockawayrecycling.com/scrap-metal-prices/); [Scrap-Monitor](https://scrap-monitor.com/prices/light-iron-shredder/)).

Taken together, the defaults should change as follows. **Drop the couch fee** and the general appliance fee, keeping an optional per-piece fee for operators whose transfer station charges one (Rochester charges $40 per sofa). Set mattresses at $20 per piece, with a state setting that lowers the fee to $0–$10 in the four recycling-program states and raises it to $40–$50 in Massachusetts. Set refrigerant at $35, TVs and monitors at $25, and old tube TVs at $40. Add tires at $15 each plus $5–$10 if still on the rim, noting that Colorado and California limit unregistered haulers to 9 tires per trip ([Larimer County proposed fees](https://www.larimer.gov/sites/default/files/proposed-fees_0.pdf); [Moreno Valley](https://www.moval.org/resident_services/waste/recycle-tires.html)), and add propane cylinders at about $10.

Hot tubs, pianos and safes need their own priced line, with their volume left out of the load curve, because their cost is labor, cutting and access rather than dump fees; a 1,000-lb piano costs only about $32 to dump at $63 a ton. A reasonable default for a hot tub is $400. Angi gives $150–$800, operator videos show $300–$600, and one hauler charges $425 plus $100 per enclosed side ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-would-it-cost-remove-large-jacuzzi-tub.htm); [JM Junk](https://jmjunkremovers.com/hot-tub-removal-how-much-does-it-cost/)). An upright piano should default to $350 against an observed $175–$675 ([JM Junk](https://jmjunkremovers.com/piano-removal-pricing/); [Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/piano-removal-cost)). Safes are best priced by weight band, from $95–$175 under 200 lb to $250–$500 at 500–1,000 lb ([Readiload](https://readiload.com/safe-removal)). The operator should always confirm these items before the quote goes out.

### Priority 3: Quote a range that leans toward the items nobody photographed

The industry norm is a range from photos and a firm price on site before loading. In a competitor's mystery shop of 50 companies, only **14% gave a firm price upfront**, 46% would price only after a visit, and 22% gave a rough phone range ([Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/we-called-50-junk-removal-companies)). 1-800-GOT-JUNK says volume is hard to judge from photos and prices multi-item jobs on site ([1-800-GOT-JUNK](https://www.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/how-our-pricing-works)). Junkluggers guarantees the final charge cannot exceed the written estimate ([Junkluggers](https://www.junkluggers.com/how-it-works/junk-removal-pricing/)), and DMV Go Junk honors its photo price unless the job grows ([DMV Go Junk](https://dmvgojunk.com/cost-calculator.html)). A photo range that holds up is therefore a selling point. Errors run mostly one way, up: the common failures are items outside the frame, the second room the customer forgot, dense materials that look small, and stairs or long carries nobody mentioned ([Remoovit](https://blog.remoovit.com/2026/01/20/how-to-get-an-accurate-junk-removal-estimate-from-photos/); [GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services)). JRA's own hedge on big jobs is to quote 12 truckloads when it estimates 10 ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/)).

HaulCalc's low–high output should keep the AI's volume and weight range but change in four ways. First, it should **raise the high end by 10% of volume by default, and by 20% for multi-room jobs, cleanouts or low AI confidence**. These figures are this report's judgment, drawn from JRA's 20% round-up and one vendor's advice to accept a 10–15% miss. Second, it should state its assumptions plainly: visible items only, household-weight junk, ground-level access, and the minimum still applies if the load comes in smaller. Third, it should give operators an optional commitment: "final price confirmed on site before loading; we won't exceed the top of this range unless items are added." Fourth, it should send estates, hoarder homes and basements whose back wall isn't visible to an on-site estimate, because "the chance of underquoting from a photo is too high" ([Swivl](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-estimate)).

The intake flow should require at least two wide shots of each pile with something of known size in frame, such as a door or trash bin. It should also require close-ups of fee-bearing or heavy items, one photo of the access route and one of where the truck parks ([Remoovit](https://blog.remoovit.com/2026/01/20/how-to-get-an-accurate-junk-removal-estimate-from-photos/); [The OC Bros](https://theocbros.com/junk-removal-cost-by-state/)). Some questions belong in every quote, not only when the AI notices something: other rooms, closets or an attic; the floor, flights of stairs and elevator; the carry distance (under 50 ft, 50–100 ft, or more); truck parking and any HOA or building rules; what is inside boxes and bags (light items, or books, tools and paper); wet items; hazardous items; and the desired date and time. The box-contents question is this report's inference from the weight evidence; no operator source lists it.

AI photo estimators claim **80–95% agreement with on-site quotes**, and a moving-industry tool reports 85% ([Medium/MeetCyber](https://medium.com/meetcyber/best-ai-powered-junk-removal-software-in-2026-photo-based-estimating-vs-on-site-quotes-d5471c9d6d93); [AIONX](https://aionx.co/trade-services/best-ai-junk-removal/); [Yembo](https://yembo.ai/moving/ai-surveys)), but none of these claims has been independently checked. The one accuracy lever the vendors agree on is learning from the operator's own past jobs. HaulCalc should record the final load size, final price and dump-ticket weight for every job, then adjust its volume bias and weight-per-yard assumptions by job type.

### Priority 4: Let weight and payload, not volume, govern dense loads

A 7x14 dump trailer rated at 14,000 lb total weight carries about **9,400–10,200 lb of cargo**, according to 2026 dealer listings ([Load Runner Trailers](https://loadrunnertrailers.com/en/inventory/6a2ae80fe5f1b41efd94bf0e)). Broken concrete weighs about 2,000 lb per cubic yard ([Budget Dumpster](https://www.budgetdumpster.com/resources/dumpster-weight-calculator.php)), so the trailer reaches its weight limit at **about 4.5–5 yd³, a third of its volume**. 1-800-GOT-JUNK limits dirt and concrete to one foot deep even in a full truck ([1-800-GOT-JUNK FAQ](https://www.site2.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/frequently-asked-questions)). The 15-yd³ default also assumes 4-ft sides; a 7x14 with 2-ft sides holds about 7.3 yd³ ([EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/how-many-yards-can-a-7x14-dump-trailer-hold/)), and adding side boards raises volume but not the weight rating ([Nationwide Trailers](https://nationwidetrailers.com/resources/dump-trailer-side-extensions/)). The app should therefore calculate volume from the operator's own inside length, width and side height, and add a payload setting.

HaulCalc's weight logic, an included weight plus an overage charge per ton, mirrors operator practice; Johnny Mac's includes 500 lb per quarter load and charges $150 a ton, prorated, above that ([Johnny Mac's](https://www.johnnymacsjunkremoval.com/junk-removal-pricing)). Four changes would make it hold up. Express the allowance as **200 lb per cubic yard of load**, so it scales when the operator changes trailers. Raise the overage to **$150 a ton by default and never less than twice the per-ton rate the operator pays for that material**; at King County's $243 a ton, today's $100 overage loses money on every pound. Count loads by weight as well as volume, taking whichever is larger: volume divided by trailer capacity, or weight divided by payload. And add loading time for heavy loads, since the cost model's loading time scales only with volume and so understates shoveling concrete; 15 extra minutes per ton above the included weight is a reasonable placeholder until operators calibrate it, because no source measured it. On a quarter trailer of broken concrete (3.75 yd³, about 7,500 lb), today's settings quote about $540 and the recommended settings about $760. One Seattle hauler's dense-material prices of $89–$200 per cubic yard imply about $335–$750 for the same pile ([Seattle Rubbish](https://seattlerubbish.com/price-list/)).

Dump rates for dense materials differ from household rates in both directions, so the cost model needs a rate adjustment by material. For mixed construction debris, most landfills charge the same as or less than for household trash, according to a vendor summary of EREF data ([Dumpster Rental Systems](https://www.dumpsterrentalsystems.com/understanding-tipping-fees-highlights-from-the-2025-eref-report)), but some Northeast sites charge far more: $200 a ton at Albany's gate and $300 in Dartmouth, MA ([Albany](http://www.albanylandfill.com/content/tippingfees.cfm); [Dartmouth](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/10280835/Massachusetts/Bristol-County/Dartmouth/Board-raises-transfer-station-fees-citing-rising-C-and-D-disposal-costs-and-long-term-capital-needs)). Clean concrete, brick and dirt are usually cheaper; Oneida-Herkimer, NY charges $25 a ton for clean fill against $58–$115 for construction debris ([OHSWA](https://www.ohswa.org/assets/2026-Adopted-Fee-Schedule_20260903_emb.pdf)). There are exceptions, such as Howard County, MD, which charges $132 a ton for clean dirt ([Howard County](https://www.howardcountymd.gov/bureau-environmental-services/resource/alpha-ridge-landfill-user-fees-and-charges)). Unsorted loads are often penalized: Summit County, CO charges $150 a ton for unsorted construction debris against $85 sorted ([Waste Today](https://www.wastetodaymagazine.com/news/colorado-county-landfill-to-implement-tip-fee-for-unsorted-candd-materials)). Operators should also be able to choose a policy for dense material: quote it with the overage, send it for review, or decline it. Big Rob's never puts concrete or dirt in its trailers and passes those jobs to dump-truck partners ([Big Rob's](https://bigrobshauling.com/dumpster-rentals/dump-trailer-vs-junk-removal)).

### Priority 5: Complete the cost basis before trusting the profit check

HaulCalc's cost model leaves out several costs that operators and their advisers count. It counts only the round trip to the customer, not the detour to the dump, the line and unloading. It has no allowance for payroll tax and workers' comp (about +15–35% on wages), and it does not count the owner's own hours, even though JRA warns that if you work the truck, "you do need to account for your time" ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/)). It omits card processing at about 2–2.6% of the ticket ([ContractorToolStack](https://contractortoolstack.com/software/housecall-pro/pricing/)). And its $25 per job of overhead is about a third of the $60–$100 that insurance, vehicle payments and marketing actually come to. The stairs factor is light as well: 15% extra time per flight, against the franchisor's "can double" for stairs, long carries and no elevator combined.

The table re-prices the same jobs on a fuller cost basis: crew paid $24 an hour after payroll costs, 45 minutes and 10 extra miles for a dedicated dump run (or one-third of that when three small jobs share a run), $75 overhead and 2.5% card fees. The dump-run time and mileage are placeholders this report chose, not measured figures.

Two conclusions follow. First, even at the recommended prices, **1/8 loads and single items pay only when they share the day and the dump run with other jobs**, and better still when the customer is a repeat or referral rather than a paid lead. Removing the $35 or so of per-job marketing turns the shared 1/8 load to about +8%. This fits JRA's warning that "you're going to make very little money on a minimum load job." The profit check should therefore ask whether the dump run is shared and whether the lead was paid, rather than charging every job a full trip. Second, once costs are complete, the right **target margin is about 30%**, with 20% acceptable early on, not 50%. That matches JRA's 30% target and the 30–35% in vendor worked examples ([Zarla](https://www.zarla.com/guides/junk-removal-pricing)). Even with complete costs, cost-plus mode would quote about $335 for a 1/8 load and $525 for a full one, still the wrong shape. It works best as a minimum-price check and as the pricing method for unusual jobs such as hourly cleanouts, dense debris and out-of-area work.

Vehicle cost can stay at $0.75 a mile as long as truck payments and insurance sit in overhead. At fall-2026 prices (gas $4.16–$4.46 a gallon, diesel at a record of about $6.53), fuel alone runs about $0.36–$0.48 a mile for a gas pickup towing ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/archive/2026/2026_09_10/pdf/highlights.pdf)). For comparison, the IRS rate is 72.5¢ a mile for 2026, raised to 76¢ from July ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-sets-2026-business-standard-mileage-rate-at-725-cents-per-mile-up-25-cents); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/16/rising-fuel-costs-prompt-irs-to-raise-standard-mileage-rates-for-rest-of-2026/186784/)), and AAA's all-in cost for a half-ton pickup is 98.5¢ ([AAA](https://newsroom.aaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/UPDATE-AAA-Fact-Sheet-Your-Driving-Cost-9.2025-1.pdf)). Use $1.00–$1.25 only if vehicle payments are left out of overhead, so they are never counted twice. All rates should carry the date they were entered, with a prompt to update them: dump fees rose 10% in 2024 alone, and the College Hunks franchisor calls holding prices flat while dump fees rise "a slow-motion pay cut" ([College Hunks Franchise](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/)). Finally, add a check on revenue per truck-hour, which is how owners and business brokers judge pricing. One broker puts a healthy truck at $800–$1,500 a day ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/junk-removal-business-valuation/)), and one forum hauler reports about $140 an hour per rig ([LawnSite](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/does-anyone-offer-junk-hauling.120235/)). A $125-an-hour target flags jobs that tie up a crew for too little.

### Priority 6: Tune access, travel and timing modifiers

Keep the stairs fee at **$25 per flight**. Published stair fees run $25–$75 per flight ([Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-cost-calculator)), but Thumbtack's 2024 averages differ by only $8 between jobs with and without stairs ([Thumbtack](https://www.thumbtack.com/p/junk-removal-cost)), which suggests many operators absorb stairs on small jobs. Double the fee only for loads over half a trailer or items over about 200 lb, and raise the cost model's stairs time from 15% to 25% per flight. Add a long-carry fee of $50 per 50 ft beyond the first 50 ft, against published figures of $30–$150 ([Trash Wizard](https://trashwizard.com/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost-holden-ma/); [Homeaglow](https://www.homeaglow.com/hub/content/junk-removal-cost)).

The travel fee is too low per mile. Each extra mile of distance means two miles of driving, which at HaulCalc's own defaults costs about **$3.80 in vehicle and crew time** ($4.25 with payroll costs) against the $2 charged. Operators include travel within a **25–50-mile radius** and raise the minimum beyond it; JRA applies a full-load minimum at 51–75 miles and two full loads beyond that ([JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/when-to-charge-a-customer-extra-for-their-junk-removal-service/)). One Maryland operator includes travel within 35 miles and markets "no hidden fees" against crews that add fuel surcharges at the door ([Always Recovering Junk](https://alwaysrecoveringjunk.com/2026/04/28/hidden-fees-in-junk-removal-and-why-we-dont-have-them/)). The recommended default is a 25-mile free radius and $4 per mile beyond it, with an option to use JRA's stepped out-of-area minimum instead. Any travel charge should appear in the quote, never on arrival.

Timing and job-type options should start switched off. Same-day service can carry +$50, against published premiums of $50–$150 or 10–25% ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/junk-removal-pricing/)). After-hours or weekend work can carry 1.2 times the price, against published premiums of 10–20% and up to 1.5–2 times ([Homeaglow](https://www.homeaglow.com/hub/content/junk-removal-cost)). A curbside discount should stay off by default, because sources disagree between about 25% and a flat $5. Hoarder or heavy-sorting jobs can take +25% ([Junk Rescue](https://junk-rescue.com/blog-hoarder-cleanout/)) or switch to hourly pricing at about $60 per person-hour plus disposal, against a published $50–$120 ([Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-cost-per-hour)). Demolition of sheds ($3.50–$11.50 per sq ft) and decks ($5–$15 per sq ft) should go to manual quoting ([Junk Masterz](https://junkmasterzca.com/shed-removal-cost/); [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/deck-removal-cost)).

## Official dump fees are firm; margins and AI accuracy are not

The research environment blocked direct page loads for every site the researchers tried, so every figure in this report comes from search-engine excerpts and summaries rather than full pages. Some attributions between similar vendor pages, such as GreenRoute and DoorstepHQ, could not be pinned down. Reddit's r/junkremoval and r/sweatystartup could not be searched at all, and YouTube videos and podcasts (Sweaty Startup, Strong Back Nation, JunkBlitzPRO) were visible only as titles and descriptions, with no transcripts. The voice of working independent owners is therefore thin. It rests heavily on JRA, a coaching firm whose pricing pages are four to eight years old, plus a few forum threads (LawnSite posts, many 15–20 years old) and interviews with owners who have since scaled.

The firmest numbers are official: government dump-fee schedules for several dozen counties and cities, EREF's landfill averages read through trade press, federal and state rules on refrigerant, e-waste, mattress and paint programs, EIA fuel prices, the IRS mileage rate, BLS wages, and weight-per-yard factors such as Florida's figure measured on 171 construction-debris loads. Even these have rough edges. Some schedules are undated or contradict themselves, EREF's $62.28 also appears as a 2023 Pacific figure, and coverage of EREF's 2025 increase says both 2% and 4%. EPA lists concrete at 860 lb per cubic yard, which contradicts the roughly 2,000 lb operators observe for broken concrete; this report uses the operator figure.

The price-curve shape rests on about a dozen published independent price lists and two Two Men and a Truck location pages. These are real prices, but a small, unrepresentative and mostly undated sample, and trailer sizes were often unconfirmed. Franchise tier prices are weaker still: none of the big brands publishes a price list, and aggregators' estimates for the same 1-800-GOT-JUNK half truck range from $325 to $800 ([The Moving Master](https://themovingmaster.com/blogs/how-much-does-1-800-got-junk-cost-a-complete-2025-pricing-guide/); [VanLinesMove](https://www.vanlinesmove.com/movinganswers/minimum-price-for-1-800-got-junk)). Margin figures are the softest numbers in the report, running from Jobber's "at least 15% net" to a competitor's claimed 60% gross ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-start-a-junk-removal-business/); [Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/is-junk-removal-a-good-business)). JRA's 30% target and the scaled owners' 18–20% are the most credible. AI photo-accuracy claims (80–95%) are all vendor marketing with no independent test.

Several conflicts remain unresolved. On curve steepness, JRA's ratios (a quarter load at 42% of full, a half load at 67%) are steeper than Junk Tug's 29% and 53%. On stairs, per-flight fees of $25–$75 sit against Thumbtack's $8 average difference between jobs with and without stairs. Curbside discounts range from about 25% in one calculator to LoadUp's flat $5. Jobs per truck per day range from 1.5–3 in a vendor business plan to "5+" from a KPI site that contradicts itself. Item sizes vary too, with a queen mattress put at anywhere from 1 to 2.2 yd³ and a washer at 0.5 to 1 yd³. College HUNKS' reported $99 dispatch fee and $399 minimum could not be verified.

Nothing in the sources measures loading time by fraction of a load, time spent at the dump, miles driven per job, the payload of enclosed junk-truck bodies, or how often photo quotes miss and in which direction. Those are exactly the numbers HaulCalc can collect from its own users.

## Conclusion

HaulCalc's defaults are not wrong on average; they are wrong in shape. A typical mid-size job prices near the market, while 1/8–3/4 loads come out too cheap and single items carrying a fee come out far too expensive. An operator on these defaults therefore tends to win the small loads that lose money and lose the single-item jobs it could have won. Fixing the curve and the item fees costs little to build and changes more quotes than any other improvement.

The deeper lesson is that small-job profit depends on the route and the customer, not on volume. The same 1/8 load swings from a clear loss to modest profit depending on whether it shares a dump run and whether a paid lead brought it in, so a later version could quote small jobs more keenly on days already booked nearby. And because no published source has measured photo-quote error, loading time or tons per load, every finished job HaulCalc records improves its defaults. Over time, operators' own data will be a better guide than any of the figures in this report.
