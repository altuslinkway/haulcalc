# Customer-Facing Junk Removal Pricing in the US (Franchise vs Independent, Tiers, Minimums, Items, Surcharges)

Method note: Direct page fetches (WebFetch) were blocked by the network egress proxy for every site tried (1800gotjunk.com, junkluggers.com, homeguide.com, goloadup.com). All findings below come from web search results and the quoted snippets and summaries they returned (October 2026). The numbers are cited to the pages the search engine attributed them to, but they were not checked against the full page text. Many sources are cost-guide aggregators, review or moving-lead sites, or competitors (notably Dropcurb, LoadUp and Junkluggers comparison pages), and those sources have a commercial interest. They are flagged where relevant.

## Q1. How do the major franchises price (volume vs item, minimums, truck sizes, published price points)?

### Takeaway
Most big franchises (1-800-GOT-JUNK, College HUNKS, Junk King, Junkluggers, JDog, Two Men and a Truck junk) price by the share of a roughly 14–18 cubic-yard truck the load fills. The price is all-inclusive (labor, loading, disposal), and the quote is given on site. Typical points are a minimum or 1/8 load of about $90–$200, a half truck of about $350–$650, and a full truck of about $600–$1,100, reaching about $1,250–$1,500 in the NYC suburbs and the Bay Area. LoadUp is the main exception: it publishes instant, item-based prices by ZIP code, plus a one-time area or service fee.

### Inferences
- Franchise trucks cluster at about 14–18 cubic yards: Junkluggers about 14–16, 1-800-GOT-JUNK about 15 (unofficial), JDog 16, Junk King 18. A photo app can assume roughly 15 cubic yards per "truck" unless an operator configures otherwise, which makes 1/8 truck about 1.9 cubic yards.
- Implied franchise full-truck price per cubic yard is about $33–$75. Junk King's full $589–$658 over 18 cubic yards is about $33–$37. 1-800-GOT-JUNK's $600–$1,099 over about 14.8 cubic yards is about $41–$74. Junkluggers' $625–$1,000 over about 15 cubic yards is about $42–$67.
- Franchise structure is consistent: (a) a minimum or 1/8 tier, (b) fractional tiers in 1/8 or 1/4 steps, (c) "all-inclusive" volume pricing, (d) a final quote on site before work starts, and (e) case-by-case add-ons for heavy or special-disposal items. LoadUp's item-plus-area-fee model is the main alternative and is closest in concept to photo-based item pricing.
- Published franchise prices are scarce. Two Men and a Truck location pages are among the few official franchise pages with explicit fractional prices, and they make useful anchors for high- and low-cost markets.

### Gaps
- No official per-fraction price list was found for 1-800-GOT-JUNK, College HUNKS, Junk King, Junkluggers or JDog. Their tier numbers come from aggregators and competitors and conflict, sometimes by 2x.
- 1-800-GOT-JUNK truck dimensions are not officially confirmed.
- Only two Two Men and a Truck junk location pages were surfaced. More locations would sharpen the regional spread.
- The College HUNKS $99 dispatch fee and the Forbes Home $399 College HUNKS minimum could not be verified.

## Q2. How do independent operators structure rate cards (tiers, trailer or truck capacity, implied $/cubic yard, single-item minimums)?

### Takeaway
Independents usually publish 4–7 fractional tiers against a dump trailer or box truck: 1/8 (or 1/6), 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and full, with some adding 2/3, 5/6 or 7/8. A low "minimum" or single-item price sits below the smallest fraction. They often undercut franchises: full loads run about $380–$740 versus franchise $600–$1,100. Several add weight caps and separately listed per-item surcharges for tires and mattresses.

Tier prices are concave in volume: the minimum and small fractions carry a large share of the full price because of fixed trip costs.

- A reasonable default curve from these cards: minimum ≈ 13–20% of full, 1/8 ≈ 20–28%, 1/4 ≈ 30–36%, 1/2 ≈ 50–60%, 3/4 ≈ 75%, full = 100%.
- Implied independent $/cubic yard at full load (derived): Junk Tug $380 / 10.6 cy ≈ $36/cy. RJP $500 / 7.4 cy ≈ $68/cy (small trailer, higher per-yard). A 15-cubic-yard 7x14 trailer at Big Rob's $700 would be about $47/cy, and at Clean Green's $549 about $37/cy (trailer sizes not confirmed).
- Independent "minimum" or single-item prices are $85–$128 in these cards ($99 is common). Franchise floors are about $90–$200.
- Independents are more likely than franchises to publish exact tier prices, weight caps and explicit per-item surcharges.

### Gaps
- No Reddit (r/junkremoval, r/sweatystartup) threads or YouTube operator transcripts surfaced, so operator-reported pricing from those communities is missing.
- Trailer dimensions were not stated for Clean Green or Big Rob's, so their $/cubic yard is approximate.
- Box-truck operators' (15–20 cubic yard) published cards were not specifically found.
- Publication dates for most independent pages were not visible in search results.

## Q3. Which items carry surcharges, what are typical amounts, and are surcharges added on top of the volume price or instead of it?

### Takeaway
The common surcharge items are refrigerant appliances ($15–$60), tires ($5–$25 each), TVs and e-waste (about $25), and mattresses or box springs (about $15 as an add-on, or $65–$160 as a standalone single-item price). Hot tubs ($150–$1,100+), pianos ($99–$675) and safes ($95–$800) are usually priced as specialty items. Volume-priced companies mostly add surcharges on top of the volume price, but some fold everything into volume and others (LoadUp) price item by item instead of by volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Refrigerant (fridges, freezers, AC, dehumidifiers):**
  - A "$20 to $50 freon surcharge" is typical on most quotes because a certified technician must recover the refrigerant. — [JiffyJunk refrigerator disposal](https://www.jiffyjunk.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-dispose-of-a-refrigerator-pickup-fees-freon-surcharges-and-free-programs/)
  - Indianapolis hauler: +$25 for top-freezer fridges and freezers, +$30 for side-by-side or French-door units, +$15 for mini fridges. CRT TVs cost more. — [Indy Pickup surcharge pricing](https://www.indypickup1111.com/paid-services)
  - Cincinnati example: a second-floor fridge carried a $30 refrigerant surcharge plus a $25 stair fee. — [Cincy Junk Busters appliance cost](https://www.cincyjunkbusters.com/blog/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost-for-appliances)

## Q4. How are heavy materials (concrete, dirt, shingles, brick, tile) priced?

### Takeaway
Heavy materials break the volume model, and operators use one of four approaches: (1) volume pricing with a fill-height cap (1-800-GOT-JUNK: at most 1 ft high in a full truck), (2) a weight allowance per tier with per-ton overage ($70–$150/ton), (3) a per-cubic-yard heavy rate (about $60–$120+/cubic yard, or $89–$200/cubic yard in Seattle), or (4) quoting separately or sending the job to a dumpster or dump truck. Disposal cost is per ton, and concrete weighs about 2 tons per cubic yard, so a 15-cubic-yard trailer reaches its payload after a few yards.

### Cited Findings
- **1-800-GOT-JUNK:** for dirt, concrete and gravel, "pricing is based on the total volume that the material takes up in the truck, up to a maximum of one foot in height for a full truck" (FAQ page about 1,350 days old). — [1-800-GOT-JUNK FAQ](https://www.site2.1800gotjunk.com/us_en/frequently-asked-questions); [MoveBuddha](https://www.movebuddha.com/blog/1-800-got-junk-cost/)
- **Johnny Mac's (GA):** each tier includes weight (500 lb per 1/4 load, 2,000 lb per full load). Overage is $150/ton, prorated. — [Johnny Mac's](https://www.johnnymacsjunkremoval.com/junk-removal-pricing)
- **Big Rob's (FL):** concrete and dirt never go in its trailers. It partners with dump trucks and recyclers and quotes per job. Shingles go in a dumpster rental with 2 tons included and $70/ton after. — [Big Rob's Hauling](https://bigrobshauling.com/dumpster-rentals/dump-trailer-vs-junk-removal)
- **Junk Doctors (NC):** concrete and shingles are priced by weight, not volume. — [Junk Doctors](https://junkdrs.com/pricing/)

  - A heavy-debris 10-yard dumpster runs $350–$450 flat for up to about 10 tons of clean concrete or dirt.

### Inferences
- At about 2 tons per cubic yard, a 10,000 lb payload trailer maxes out at about 2.5 cubic yards of concrete, about 1/6 of its volume. A photo-based app must therefore detect heavy material and switch from volume tiers to a weight-based or "limited fill" mode. Otherwise a pile of broken concrete filling 1/4 of a trailer is underpriced and may exceed legal payload.
- Practical default rules: (a) heavy-material rate of about $75–$200 per cubic yard, or (b) per-tier weight allowance (about 500 lb per 1/4 load) plus $70–$150/ton overage, or (c) flag for manual quote. Shingles are typically priced above concrete because of landfill rates and bundle weight (about 3 bundles per 100 sq ft; weight not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No published flat per-load price was found for a dump trailer of concrete, dirt or shingles from a junk hauler.
- Brick and tile specific pricing was not found separately from "concrete/heavy."
- The often-cited "$120 per cubic yard for heavy, dense material" figure surfaced in a search summary without a confirmable source page.

## Q7. How do operators communicate quotes (photo ranges, on-site final price, binding, "not to exceed")?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is a range from photos, phone or online, then a final firm price on site before loading. Franchises (1-800-GOT-JUNK, College HUNKS, Junkluggers, JDog) give a free on-site estimate. Junkluggers explicitly caps the final charge at the written estimate. A growing set of independents and platforms (LoadUp, DMV Go Junk, AI-quote startups) offer instant or photo-based prices that "hold" unless scope changes, usually with fine print allowing on-site adjustment.

### Cited Findings
- **1-800-GOT-JUNK:** no exact online quotes; free on-site estimate with the price given on the spot before work; all-inclusive. Reviewers note final prices can rise on site. — [MoveBuddha](https://www.movebuddha.com/blog/1-800-got-junk-cost/); [Move.org](https://www.move.org/1-800-got-junk-review/)
- **Junkluggers:** free on-site estimate before any work. The final charge cannot climb above the written estimate, and a discount is possible if the load takes less space. — [Junkluggers pricing](https://www.junkluggers.com/how-it-works/junk-removal-pricing/); [Junkluggers FAQ](https://www.junkluggers.com/how-it-works/faqs/)
- **College HUNKS:** free estimates before starting work; no per-item prices online. — [College HUNKS Houston](https://www.collegehunkshaulingjunk.com/central-houston/); [Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/college-hunks-hauling-junk-cost)
- **JDog:** free on-site or remote estimate. — [JDog pricing](https://www.jdogjunkremoval.com/pricing/)
- **Junk King:** online quoting is partial and estimate-only (per a competitor); it will beat written competitor estimates. — [Dropcurb](https://dropcurb.com/blog/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost); [Move.org Junk King](https://www.move.org/junk-king-review/)
- **LoadUp:** instant upfront item prices by ZIP code. Its own guide also says the binding price is given by the crew on site after assessment. — [LoadUp pricing](https://goloadup.com/pricing/); [LoadUp vs College Hunks](https://goloadup.com/blog/guides/loadup-vs-college-hunks/)
- **DMV Go Junk (Northern Virginia independent):** the photo price holds unless the scope changes significantly and the customer approves the change. It factors stairs and tight hallways into photo quotes. It notes that some competitors lowball over the phone and revise on site. — [DMV Go Junk](https://dmvgojunk.com/cost-calculator.html)
- **Haul of the Wild (CO):** photo estimate as a quick starting point; confirms access and specialty items before a firm number on larger jobs. — [Haul of the Wild](https://haulofthewildservices.com/pricing/)
- **ScanJunk Removal (Stockton, CA, "AI-powered"):** terms say the price at booking is the price paid, but also that actual pricing is determined on site. Items visible in photos but not on the booking confirmation are excluded. It asks for clear, well-lit photos from several angles. — [ScanJunk terms](https://scanjunkremoval.com/terms)
- **General guidance:**
  - A quote is an estimate, not a locked contract. The binding number typically comes after the crew physically assesses the items and is presented before loading. — [American Valor Waste](https://americanvalorwaste.com/how-junk-removal-quotes-work/)
  - Reputable companies give a range from photos or phone and confirm on site. — [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-price-a-junk-removal-job/) (via search summary)
  - Recommended photos: the whole pile, a close-up, and the access route. — [ClearoutLocal](https://clearoutlocal.com/guides/prepare-and-compare/) (attribution from search summary, not verified)
  - Put binding wording just above the signature line. — [PDFEstimate quote template](https://pdfestimate.com/junk-removal-quote-template/). A hauling-software vendor recommends signed change orders for added material instead of padding the final invoice (vendor page not identified in search results).

### Inferences
- For a photo-based app, the market-standard output is a price range tied to a volume tier (for example "1/4–3/8 truck: $230–$300") plus itemized surcharges. The output should carry an explicit rule that the final price is confirmed on site before loading. An optional "won't exceed the top of the range unless items are added" commitment would match the more customer-friendly practices (Junkluggers, DMV Go Junk).
- The app should capture the access route (stairs, long carry) in photos, as operators who honor photo quotes rely on that information.

### Gaps
- No franchise was found using the literal phrase "not-to-exceed." Junkluggers' "can't exceed the written estimate" is the closest.
- The share of operators who quote binding prices from photos, versus ranges, was not quantified anywhere found.
- YouTube operator content and Reddit discussions of quoting practices did not surface in searches.
