# How Independent Junk Removal Owner-Operators Quote Jobs and What Drives Per-Job Profit (Practitioner Knowledge)

> **Source-access note for the report writer (read first).** In this research environment, direct page fetches were blocked for every practitioner site tried: junkremovalauthority.com, sweatystartup.com, ownedandoperated.com, youtube.com, reddit.com, doorstephq.com and greenrouteapp.com. Reddit could not be searched at all; the search tool rejects reddit.com. So **no YouTube transcripts, podcast transcripts or Reddit threads were read directly.** Everything below comes from search-engine excerpts and summaries of those pages, plus video titles and descriptions. Where a claim depends on a short excerpt rather than the full page, it says so. Junk Removal Authority (JRA) is a coaching and marketing firm for junk removal owners. It is the most-cited practitioner source here, but most JRA pages date from about 2019–2022, so treat its dollar figures as dated and its methods as still in use. Many 2025–2026 "how to price" pages come from software vendors (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz, QuoteIQ, Dropcurb, Swivl). They repeat operator folk wisdom but are not first-hand operator accounts, and they are labeled as vendors below.

## 1. Which pricing methods do owners use and recommend (volume, weight, time and materials, cost-plus, flat item, hybrid), and why?

### Takeaway
Practitioners mostly recommend **volume pricing**: a price ladder of truck or trailer fractions from a minimum up to a full load. Heavy materials, stairs, long carries and special-disposal items are added as surcharges. **Hourly or time-and-materials pricing** is kept for jobs whose volume is hard to predict (hoarder cleanouts, estates, labor-only work, demolition). Many small trailer haulers also use it, charging load labor plus haul plus the actual dump fee. Pure weight pricing is rare. Per-item menus are discouraged except for small jobs and special-disposal items.

### Cited Findings
**Volume (fraction of truck) is the dominant, coach-recommended method**
- JRA describes volume as the most common model: prices are set as fractions of a truck between a minimum and a full load. When a job needs more than one truckload, "the pricing simply restarts for every truckload after the first." JRA says an experienced team leader "can accurately quote load sizes after taking a look at items to be removed," which lets the company give a firm price upfront. — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt; page is several years old)
- JRA tells "all of our clients" to price by load size: "Start with your minimum price and then set a definitive price point at each 1/16th mark all the way up to a full truck." — [JRA: Two of the Biggest Pricing Mistakes](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/) (via search excerpt; ~4 years old)
- The College Hunks franchisor (a franchise system, not an independent operator) describes a pricing sheet as "a short ladder of truckload fractions." The ladder is a minimum plus ¼, ½, ¾ and full tiers, a labor-only crew-hour rate with a minimum, and a list of surcharges, "six lines and nothing more." Extra tiers slow down quoting and confuse customers. — [College Hunks Franchise: How to Build a Junk Removal Pricing Sheet](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/) (via search excerpt)
- Kyle Landwehr (Slam Dunkin' Junk, St. Louis; also runs "The Junk Academy" coaching) told Workiz that customers "want to know how much they're going to be charged upfront, so make sure you have a pricing guide that accounts for job size, item type, and location." — [Workiz: Grow Your Junk Removal Business to $20K/Month](https://www.workiz.com/blog/junk-removal/how-to-grow-your-junk-removal-business/) (vendor-hosted interview)

**Weight-based pricing: discussed, rarely used**
- JRA notes that some companies weigh the truck before and after each job and charge per pound. This makes disposal costs easier to control but "makes it a bit more difficult to quote a job before hand because you won't know the exact weight." JRA's example is Junk Rangers in Vancouver (Canada), which mounts scales on its trucks. The article is about 7.5 years old. — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt)
- A viewer comment on a YouTube pricing video makes the same practical point: without a portable scale you can't know what a given visit weighed, so weight pricing is hard to verify. — [YouTube: How To Price Junk Removal & Win Your Bids!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E-VrP6neyPw) (listing/comment excerpt only; channel name not retrievable)

**Hybrid (volume plus labor)**
- JRA: hybrid operators "have a base rate for the volume of junk removal. Then they will add on a labor fee on top of that based on the amount of work to get the truck loaded." — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt)
- JRA's large-job method is also a hybrid: truckloads × per-load rate, plus overage hours × hourly rate. See Q2. — [JRA: How to Price Large Junk Removal Jobs](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/) (via search excerpt)

**Time and materials or cost-build (common among small trailer and landscaper haulers)**
- LawnSite forum posters (landscapers who also haul junk) build up the charge in pieces. One charges at least $125 to load, $100 to haul, plus the actual dump fee, which they look up ahead of time. Another suggests $75/hr with a 3–4 hour minimum plus dump fees, or $125 per load plus dump fees. Concrete, shingles and construction debris get a higher fee. Chemicals cost extra because they must be separated and taken to a different facility. Many of these posts are **15–20 years old**. — [LawnSite: Does anyone offer junk hauling?](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/does-anyone-offer-junk-hauling.120235/); [LawnSite: junk removal](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/junk-removal.229722/); [LawnSite: What do you charge for disposal?](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/what-do-you-charge-for-disposal.516545/) (via search summary)
- JRA's advice for concrete, dirt and heavy demolition is to sub it out to "somebody who does all the work or a guy with a dump truck." Its example subcontractor bills **$75/hour** from the time he starts driving to the job until his last dump and the drive home, plus disposal. — [JRA: How To Price Your Junk Removal Services (video)](https://junkremovalauthority.com/video/how-to-price-your-junk-removal-services/) (via search excerpt; partly garbled transcript)

**Cost-plus (vendor framing of the same logic)**
- Jobber's formula is Price = Cost ÷ (1 – target margin). Its example: a $400 cost at a 30% margin gives a $571.43 price. Jobber also suggests adding about 20% to hourly labor cost for taxes, insurance and similar expenses. — [Jobber Academy: How to Price a Junk Removal Job](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-price-a-junk-removal-job/) (vendor)
- Housecall Pro: "Price based on real costs: Include labor, disposal, drive time, and overhead—not just truck space." It says hourly pricing suits "sorting-heavy jobs, hoarding situations, estate cleanouts, or commercial jobs where the final volume is hard to predict." — [Housecall Pro: Junk Removal Pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/junk-removal-pricing/) (vendor)

**Flat item pricing: discouraged except for small jobs and special-disposal items**
- Vendor guides note that item pricing "trains customers to nickel-and-dime you." They advise keeping per-item add-ons for items with special disposal rules (refrigerant appliances, tires, mattresses, TVs and monitors). A loveseat and a sectional are "completely different hauls." — [Swivl: Junk Removal Prices](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-prices); [Billbooks: How to Price Junk Removal Jobs](https://www.billbooks.com/blog/how-to-price-junk-removal-jobs/) (vendors, via search summary)

### Inferences
- The core practitioner model is a **front-loaded volume ladder** with **surcharges** for weight, access and special items. Hourly pricing is a fallback for unpredictable or labor-dominated jobs. A HaulCalc-style tool should probably output a volume tier price and show the surcharges and an hourly cross-check alongside it.
- Weight is handled as an exception (a heavy-debris surcharge, a separate quote, or "includes X tons"), not as the main pricing unit. The likely reason is that operators can't weigh on site.

### Gaps
- Could not read any Reddit (r/junkremoval, r/sweatystartup) threads or YouTube transcripts to quantify how many independent operators use each method. No survey data on method prevalence was found.
- Could not confirm whether well-known YouTube operators (e.g., the channels behind "How We Price Junk Removal Jobs" and "HOW TO PRICE JUNK REMOVAL CLEANOUTS") prefer volume or hourly pricing. Only titles and fragments of descriptions were visible: [YouTube: How We Price Junk Removal Jobs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IVH1sUsLQ4w), [YouTube: HOW TO PRICE JUNK REMOVAL CLEANOUTS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KmlOKcKYwlE).

## 2. What rules of thumb do they use (revenue per cubic yard, dump fee × X, minimum job price, hourly revenue per truck, target margin per job, revenue per day per truck)?

### Takeaway
The most-repeated practitioner rules come from JRA:
- Getting a truck to a site costs about **$70–$80**, so the minimum must clear that.
- Load fractions should be priced **non-linearly**: in JRA's example a ¼ load is about 42% of the full price and a ½ load about 67%.
- Target about **30% margin** per job, and accept around 20% when starting out.
- On big jobs, quote high and expect the per-yard price to fall as volume grows.

Broker and vendor content puts a healthy truck at about **$800–$1,500+ revenue per working day**. Typical published rates are about **$30–$65 per cubic yard**. The minimum is commonly **$75–$150**, and a full truck **$400–$875** depending on market and box size. No operator-sourced "dump fee × X" rule was found. One side-hustle blog uses "dump fee × 2–3 + labor."

### Cited Findings
**Minimum job price and the show-up cost**
- JRA estimates that getting a truck to a site costs about **$70**. A minimum near **$95** would then leave roughly $20 profit, and JRA warns "you're going to make very little money on a minimum load job." — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt; dated)
- In another article JRA puts the show-up cost at about **$80** for most companies and says that is why the early increments must carry more of the price. — [JRA: Two of the Biggest Pricing Mistakes](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/) (via search excerpt)
- A JRA video lists a **$95 minimum** up to a **$490 full truck**, all labor included. The metadata suggests it is about 7 years old. — [JRA video: How to Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/video/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt)
- Vendor and operator guides commonly put the minimum at **$75–$150** "just to show up and take something small" for solo operators. — [Billbooks](https://www.billbooks.com/blog/how-to-price-junk-removal-jobs/) (vendor, via search summary). Housecall Pro's 2026 range is **$70–$150**. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/junk-removal-pricing/)
- A Kansas City trailer operator (15-yd trailer) charges a **$150 minimum** that **includes one ton of disposal**. — [Dump It KC](https://dumpitkc.com/trash-and-junk-removal-services-in-kansas-city) (operator pricing page, via search summary)

**Non-linear load-fraction ladder**
- JRA's example prices were **full $490, ½ $330, ¼ $205**. That makes ¼ ≈ 42% and ½ ≈ 67% of the full price. JRA says to pick a full-load price and apply those ratios. — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt)
- JRA warns: "Do not simply take your full truck load price and quarter it to get your ¼ load price, or divide it in half to get your ½ load price… weight your increases heavier toward the beginning of your load size scale. Because the majority of your junk removal expenses are incurred showing up on the job site." — [JRA: Two of the Biggest Pricing Mistakes](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/) (via search excerpt)
- An Arizona operator's all-inclusive (labor, fuel, dump) ladder is **¼ $110, ½ $200, ¾ $290, full $380** for a load of about 10.6 yd. Here ¼ ≈ 29% and ½ ≈ 53% of full, flatter than JRA's ratios. — [Junk Tug pricing page](https://junktug.com/junk-removal-pricing) (operator pricing page, via search summary; operator identity inferred from the result list)
- A Colorado operator using a ~14–15 yd dump trailer: **¼ trailer $275–$500, ½ $425–$575+, ¾ $650–$725, full $800–$875+**. Heavy debris is quoted separately because disposal is weight-based. — [Haul of the Wild Services pricing](https://haulofthewildservices.com/pricing/) (operator pricing page, via search summary)

**Revenue per cubic yard**
- Published guides put standard household junk at about **$30–$65 per cubic yard**. — [Method.me pricing guide](https://www.method.me/pricing-guides/how-much-to-charge-for-junk-removal/) (vendor). HomeGuide's **$1.50/cu ft ≈ $40.50/yd**. — [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/junk-removal-prices) (consumer cost guide)
- JRA says the per-cubic-yard price usually **drops as jobs get larger** "because you have those fixed costs to get out there." JRA also suggests converting competitors' quotes to a per-yard rate and pricing "a bit higher," because franchises tend to estimate low. — [JRA: How to Price Large Junk Removal Jobs](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/); [JRA: Two of the Biggest Pricing Mistakes](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/) (via search excerpts)

**Large-job rule**
- JRA's worked example: **10 truckloads × $600 + 10 overage hours × $50 = $6,500**. Because big-job estimates tend to be off, JRA suggests quoting it as **12 truckloads ($8,200)** rather than lower. — [JRA: How to Price Large Junk Removal Jobs](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/) (via search excerpt)

**"Dump fee × X"**
- **No operator or coach source** stating a dump-fee multiplier was found.
- The closest is a side-hustle blog: take the per-load disposal cost × **2–3**, then add labor. Its example is a $60 half-load dump fee plus 2 hrs × $50 = a **$250 minimum price**. It estimates about **$130–$170 actual profit** on a $250 job after gas and dump fees. — [MoneyPantry: Dump Trailer Side Hustle](https://moneypantry.com/make-money-with-dump-trailer/) (blog, not an operator)
- A waste-company blog says haulers typically mark up tipping fees by only about 15–30%, for example $70/ton becoming $80–$90/ton on an invoice. That describes disposal pass-through, not a pricing multiplier. — [National Waste: What Landfill Tipping Fees Really Cost](https://www.nationalwaste.com/blog/what-landfill-tipping-fees-really-cost-hidden-markups-and-regional-differences/) (self-interested source)

**Revenue per truck per day / hourly revenue**
- A business broker treats revenue per truck per working day as the main screen buyers use. It puts a healthy full-time truck at **$800–$1,500+/day**. Trucks under $800/day lead buyers to assume weak booking density, underpricing or idle trucks. — [CT Acquisitions: Junk Removal Business Valuation](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/junk-removal-business-valuation/) (broker)
- A KPI site sets a target of **5+ jobs per truck per day** and $800–$1,500 per truck daily. — [Pulse RevOps: Junk Removal KPIs](https://pulserevops.com/industry-kpis/ik0440). Low reliability: the site appears to be auto-generated and its own figures are internally inconsistent.
- A single-truck owner-operator model projects **$8,000–$18,000/month** from 30–70 jobs, or **1.5–3 jobs per working day**. — [Dropcurb: Junk Removal Business Plan](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-business-plan) (vendor). At 22–26 working days that is roughly **$310–$820/truck/day**; this is search-tool arithmetic, so many solo trucks sit below the broker's $800 floor.
- Operator YouTube titles present **$1,400–$1,500 days** as content-worthy days: "How I Make $1,500 A Day With This Easy Junk Removal Pricing Tactic" (Jobber-sponsored; walks through two real jobs) and "$1,400 Junk Removal Day: Real Jobs, Real Numbers." — [YouTube TQr9wEta6fk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQr9wEta6fk); [YouTube RI_aqK_XsKc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RI_aqK_XsKc) (titles/descriptions only; channel names not retrievable)
- A LawnSite poster charges **$350 per trailer load**, taking about **2.5 hours** including loading and the landfill run, or roughly **$140/hr gross** per rig. — [LawnSite: Does anyone offer junk hauling?](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/does-anyone-offer-junk-hauling.120235/) (via search summary; post date unclear, thread is long-running)
- Hourly crew rates quoted by vendor/consumer guides: **$75–$125/hr** for standard jobs and **$125–$150+/hr** for large cleanouts or heavy debris, typically for 2–3 workers plus truck and basic disposal. — [Dropcurb: Junk Removal Cost Per Hour](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-cost-per-hour) (vendor, via search summary)

**Target margin per job**
- JRA: "You just need to figure out all of your expenses throughout an entire job and you're going to make about **30%** on it. Maybe you accept a little bit less at the start. Maybe **20%**." If you work the truck yourself, "you do need to account for your time." — [JRA: How To Set Your Junk Removal Pricing](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) (via search excerpt)

### Inferences
- A defensible practitioner-grounded default ladder takes JRA's ratios (¼ ≈ 40–45%, ½ ≈ 65–70% of full) and puts a floor under the minimum. The floor should be at least the show-up cost (dated $70–$80, which a 2026 tool should inflate) plus a profit buffer.
- For trailer operators, per-yard rates of about $35–$60/yd applied to actual trailer volume yield full-trailer prices of roughly $380–$650 for a ~10–11 yd 7×14. Larger 14–15 yd dump trailers in higher-cost markets reach $800–$875 (Haul of the Wild).
- Several metrics (revenue per truck-day, $/hr per rig) all point the same way: practitioners judge pricing by **daily or hourly yield per truck**, not by the margin on any single job.

### Gaps
- No operator-sourced "dump fee × X" rule exists in the accessible material. This is likely forum folklore that could not be verified because Reddit was inaccessible.
- JRA's dollar figures ($95 min, $490 full) are 4–7 years old. No 2025–2026 JRA rate card was accessible.
- No independent operator's published "target $/hr per truck" was found beyond the LawnSite anecdote and the broker's per-day band.

## 3. Labor: crew size, wages (2024–2026), loading times, effect of stairs/carries/disassembly, and pricing labor-heavy jobs (demolition, hoarder cleanouts, hot tubs)

### Takeaway
Coach economics assume a **two-person crew per truck**. A second person reportedly cuts loading time by about 35–45% on a typical residential load. Helpers earn about **$16–$25/hr** (2026 postings). Budget a loaded cost of the wage plus about 20–35%. Most residential jobs take **30 min–2 hrs** on site, and garage or basement cleanouts take **2–4 hrs**. A "one-hour" job is often about **three paid crew-hours** door to door. Stairs, long carries and no elevator can about **double load-out time** for the same volume. Labor-heavy jobs are priced hourly, or as volume plus an overage hourly fee. Hot tubs commonly run **$300–$600**.

### Cited Findings
**Crew size and labor accounting**
- JRA's per-job economics assume **two crew members on each junk hauling truck**. The labor line counts only truck labor, not management or receptionist pay, and it "includes the drive time, load time, and dump time for the truck crew." — [JRA: How Much Money are You Making on Each Junk Removal Job?](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-much-money-are-you-making-on-each-junk-removal-job/) (via search excerpt; ~4.8 years old)
- A hiring guide estimates a second person saves about **35–45%** of the time on a typical residential load, with more on team lifts and much more on stairs. New helpers need **2–4 weeks** of training before they speed up. Loaded labor cost is the wage **+25–35%**. — [Junk Removal 365: Hiring Your First Junk Removal Crew Member](https://junkremoval365.com/hiring-your-first-junk-removal-crew-member/) (via search summary; operator-education site)
- Jobber: add about **20%** to hourly labor cost for taxes and insurance. — [Jobber Academy](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/junk-removal/how-to-price-a-junk-removal-job/) (vendor)

**Wages (2025–2026)**
- General laborer on the truck: **$16–$24/hr**, higher in expensive metros or for someone who can drive and lead a job. — [Junk Removal 365](https://junkremoval365.com/hiring-your-first-junk-removal-crew-member/) (via search summary)
- ZipRecruiter's national average is **$17.52/hr** (June 22, 2026), with most earning **$15.38–$19.23**. Live postings show a Helper/Driver at **$19–$25/hr** and a laborer at **$20–$25/hr**. — [ZipRecruiter: Junk Removal Salary](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Junk-Removal-Salary); [ZipRecruiter: Junk Removal Jobs](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Junk-Removal)
- Glassdoor's **$37/hr** is an outlier, probably blending owner and manager titles. — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/junk-removal-salary-SRCH_KO0,12.htm)

**Loading time and access effects**
- Most small jobs take **30 minutes–2 hours**. Mid-size garage or basement cleanouts typically take **2–4 hours**. A two-person crew handles most residential jobs efficiently. — [Easy Cleanouts LLC: How Long Does Junk Removal Take?](https://easycleanoutsllc.com/blog/how-long-does-junk-removal-take/) (operator blog, via search summary)
- "A 'one-hour' job is often three hours of paid crew time door to door," once you count the drive to the customer, loading, the haul to the transfer station, waiting in the dump line, unloading and the drive back. — vendor guides [DoorstepHQ](https://doorstephq.com/blog/how-to-estimate-junk-removal-jobs-without-losing-money) / [GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services) (via search summary; which of the two said this was not confirmed)
- A solo-operator model allows **1 hour of loading** within a **3-hour job** that includes dump runs. — [Durable: How to start a junk removal business in 72 hours](https://durable.com/guides/how-to-start-junk-removal-business) (vendor)
- The College Hunks franchisor says stairs, long carries and no elevator access "can double load-out time on the same volume." Crew labor depends more on load-out time than on the space the load fills. — [College Hunks Franchise: Pricing Sheet](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/) (via search excerpt)
- Stairs surcharges: **$25–$75 per flight** (Dropcurb); stairs or difficult access **+$50–$150** (Homeaglow). — [Dropcurb: Junk Removal Estimate](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-estimate); [Homeaglow cost guide](https://www.homeaglow.com/hub/content/junk-removal-cost) (via search summary)
- Wexford's example: a **$450 job finished quickly can out-earn a $350 job that takes twice as long**. — [Wexford Insurance: How to Price Junk Removal](https://www.wexfordins.com/post/how-to-price-junk-removal) (insurer blog)

**Labor-heavy jobs**
- *Large cleanouts:* JRA uses truckloads × load price plus **overage hours at $50/hr** (the 10-load example in Q2). — [JRA: How to Price Large Junk Removal Jobs](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/) (via search excerpt)
- *Hoarder cleanouts:* Housecall Pro recommends hourly pricing with a **minimum number of hours** that covers travel, setup and admin. Basic trash-outs can be as low as **$35 per employee-hour** plus job costs. — [Palmetto Commercial Services](https://palmettocommercialservices.com/hoarder-cleaning-columbia-sc/) (operator page, via search summary)
- Some hoarding cleanups combine a **per-square-foot fee with an hourly labor rate**. — consumer guides, e.g. [HomeGuide: Hoarding Cleanup Cost](https://homeguide.com/costs/hoarding-cleanup-cost) (via search summary)
- *Heavy single items:* the College Hunks franchisor lists safes, pianos, hot tubs, treadmills and riding mowers as justifying a price above the ladder. — [College Hunks Franchise](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/)

  - Another operator "usually charge[s] **$300**" for demo and haul-off. — [YouTube: Hot Tub removal cost is $300](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7lC2ioA2W3g)

### Inferences
- For a quoting tool, labor should be modeled as **crew-hours** (on-site load time scaled by access multipliers, plus drive and dump time), not as a function of volume alone. The practitioner evidence for an access multiplier is roughly **2× for stairs, long carries or no elevator** (College Hunks franchisor). The evidence for crew size is a **~0.55–0.65 time factor** for 2 people vs 1 (Junk Removal 365).
- At about $18–$25/hr wage plus 25–35% burden, a 2-person crew costs roughly **$45–$68 per crew-hour** before the truck and owner pay. This is arithmetic from the cited wages.

### Gaps

- No operator-sourced disassembly surcharges (e.g., swing sets, sheds, playsets) were found in the accessible material.

## 4. Travel: trip/fuel fees, service radius, and how drive time affects pricing

### Takeaway
Most operators **don't itemize travel** inside their core area. The **minimum charge absorbs the trip**, and some operators state a service radius. Outside the radius, practitioners raise the minimum instead of adding per-mile fees. JRA's rule: a 50-mile radius, a **full-load minimum at 51–75 miles**, and a **two-full-load minimum** beyond that. Fuel surcharges added at the door are a known customer complaint.

### Cited Findings
- JRA: "If a customer is outside of that standard area, you should raise your minimum price. We have a **50-mile service radius**. In some areas of the country, your service area might be **25 miles** if the population is very dense." It typically applies a **full-load minimum from about 51–75 miles**; "any farther out we adjust to a **two full load minimum**." The source is about 8 years old. — [JRA: When To Charge A Customer Extra](https://junkremovalauthority.com/when-to-charge-a-customer-extra-for-their-junk-removal-service/) (via search excerpt)
- Always Recovering Junk (Frederick, MD; 2026 post) uses a **35-mile radius** with travel included. Customers outside it are told before anything is scheduled, and the company positions "no hidden fees" as a selling point. — [Always Recovering Junk: Hidden Fees in Junk Removal](https://alwaysrecoveringjunk.com/2026/04/28/hidden-fees-in-junk-removal-and-why-we-dont-have-them/) (operator blog, via search summary)
- The same operator's blog describes the complaint pattern: the crew "shows up after an initial phone quote and adds a fuel surcharge for being outside the normal area." — [Always Recovering Junk](https://alwaysrecoveringjunk.com/2026/04/28/hidden-fees-in-junk-removal-and-why-we-dont-have-them/)
- A pricing guide lists two travel models: a **flat travel fee** covering a set radius with extra charges beyond it, or **mileage-based** pricing. Distance to both the job and the disposal facility matters because of fuel and road time. — [SJ Junk Hauling: How to Price Your Junk Removal Services](https://sjjunkhauling.com/junk-removal/how-to-price-your-junk-removal-services/) (operator blog, via search summary)
- Minimums are said to "cover travel, labor, and disposal," typically **$75–$125**, with another source putting a trip-covering minimum at **$100–$175**. — consumer and operator cost guides, e.g. [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost.htm); [GoLoadUp](https://goloadup.com/blog/guides/how-much-junk-removal-cost/) (via search summary)
- Strong Back Nation (a junk removal podcast), episode "Smart Strategies for Junk Removal Efficiency," covers how gas prices affect profit, how to choose a dump site that balances cost and convenience, and "when cheap dump fees cost you time." — [iHeart: Strong Back Nation episode](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/263-strong-back-nation-267486974/episode/smart-strategies-for-junk-removal-efficiency-269028380/) (show notes only)
- Vendor note: "small jobs cost more in drive time than they bring in," and the minimum must absorb dispatch plus the dump run. — [Swivl](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-prices) (vendor, via search summary)

### Inferences
- Practitioners treat **drive and dump time as part of crew-hours** (JRA counts drive, load and dump time in labor). Distance therefore feeds into the minimum and into whether a job is worth taking, more than into a visible line item.
- A tool could model travel as **round-trip drive minutes plus dump-site detour minutes**, converted at the loaded crew-hour plus truck cost, with a radius-based minimum step-up (JRA style).

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) operator-published per-mile rates or fuel-surcharge amounts were found. The dump-trailer rental example ($4/mile, $75 trip charge) is a rental business, not junk removal.
- No data on the typical drive time to the dump per job.

## 5. Quoting from photos/text: methods, mistakes (underestimated volume, hidden items, weight), handling on-site differences, questions asked

### Takeaway
The prevailing practice is that a **photo or text quote is a range or non-binding estimate**, and the **firm price is set on site before loading**. Many operators won't give a firm price remotely at all. Practitioners and the guides that summarize them highlight these failure modes:
- items outside the photo frame
- the "second room" or attic the customer forgot
- heavy materials that look small
- stairs and long carries not disclosed
- newer staff misjudging volume

Remedies are to ask for multiple wide shots that show pile height, ask about access and heavy items, quote one all-in number in writing, and confirm the tier before loading. A minority of operators sell "photo quote = final price" as a differentiator.

### Cited Findings
**How operators handle remote vs on-site pricing**
- Dropcurb's mystery-shop of 50 companies (15 national franchise locations, 15 regional chains, 20 independents; Feb 15–Mar 5, 2026; single-sofa scenario) found:
  - **14%** gave a firm upfront price before a visit
  - **46%** would only price after an on-site look ("We'll need to send someone out to take a look")
  - **22%** gave a broad phone range with the final charge on arrival
  - **12%** routed to a web form or callback
  - **6%** were unreachable

  — [Dropcurb: We Called 50 Junk Removal Companies](https://dropcurb.com/blog/we-called-50-junk-removal-companies) (self-published by a competitor; not independently verified)
- Junk King (franchise) says its phone and online estimates become firm only when the crew quotes on site, and a text-photo estimate is not a firm price. — [Junk King pricing](https://www.junk-king.com/pricing) (via search summary)
- Junk It Portland (independent) can usually give a **firm phone price for smaller jobs**. Larger jobs get an **estimated range** that technicians confirm on site. — [Junk It Portland: Prices](https://junkitportland.com/junk-removal-prices/) (operator page, via search summary)
- American Valor Waste: most firms settle the final price at the on-site walkthrough, and crews should not load anything until the customer approves the price. A minimum charge may still apply if the load is smaller than expected. — [American Valor Waste: How Junk Removal Quotes Work](https://americanvalorwaste.com/how-junk-removal-quotes-work/) (operator blog)
- JunkRabbit (NYC) markets "photo quote is the final price" and criticizes franchises that add fees at the door. — [JunkRabbit NYC](https://junkrabbit.nyc/blog/how-much-does-junk-removal-cost-nyc) (competitor marketing claim)
- An operator YouTube video, "DON'T PROVIDE QUOTES for your junk removal business customers over the phone!", argues "there is a HUGE difference between an estimate and a quote." It says you can give an estimate by phone if the customer understands it isn't firm. — [YouTube TL6J6PhZBNs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TL6J6PhZBNs) (description excerpt only)
- Another operator video demonstrates on-site quoting ("How I Quote My Junk Removal Jobs! - In Person Example"). — [YouTube E20viYnSToc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E20viYnSToc) (title only)
- Vendor guides recommend giving a **written range from photos or a short video**, then **confirming the exact tier on site before anything is loaded**. They advise giving **one all-in number** with surcharges included up front, "so there are no awkward upcharges later," and texting a clean itemized quote right away. — [GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services) / [DoorstepHQ](https://doorstephq.com/blog/how-to-estimate-junk-removal-jobs-without-losing-money) (vendors, via search summary; exact attribution between the two not confirmed)

**Photo-quote mistakes and what to ask for**
- "Customers routinely show you the couch and forget the second room." Check closets, attic and side areas. — [GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services) / [DoorstepHQ](https://doorstephq.com/blog/how-to-estimate-junk-removal-jobs-without-losing-money) (vendors, via search summary)
- Anything out of frame tends to be left out of the price. A garage that looked like a quick pickup can turn into a much bigger job once boxes behind the door or along the back wall come into view. Photos should show the **full pile edge to edge, its height, and how tightly items are stacked**, from more than one angle. Customers should mention **stairs, parking and long carry distances**. — [Remoovit: Accurate Junk Removal Estimate From Photos](https://blog.remoovit.com/2026/01/20/how-to-get-an-accurate-junk-removal-estimate-from-photos/) (marketplace blog, Jan 2026)
- Weight: "if you guess wrong on weight, your quote can change once the crew sees the load. Flag heavy items early." — [Remoovit: Junk Removal Cost Calculator](https://blog.remoovit.com/2025/12/27/junk-removal-cost-calculator-what-impacts-pricing-and-how-to-pay-less/). A quarter-truck of household clutter and a quarter-truck of wet drywall "take the same space, but the drywall weighs far more." Concrete, dirt, tile and brick "weigh way more than they look." — vendor guides (via search summary)
- Estimating skill varies: "more experienced team members can judge volume fairly accurately, while newer, less experienced team members will misquote." — pricing guide cited in search summary (see also [JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/) on experienced team leaders)
- JRA's big-job advice to **round the estimate up** (10 → 12 loads) is a practitioner hedge against systematic underestimation. — [JRA: How to Price Large Junk Removal Jobs](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-price-large-junk-removal-jobs/)
- On-site negotiation room: JRA notes that resale items and scrap-metal income "create more room to wiggle." On a $490 garage-cleanout full load, though, "you might not want to volunteer a discount at the start." — [JRA video: How To Price Your Junk Removal Services](https://junkremovalauthority.com/video/how-to-price-your-junk-removal-services/) (via garbled transcript excerpt)
- Customer-side evidence of upward drift: a Seattle user reported a **$140 phone quote** for a mattress that became **$198 plus tax** on arrival. — single anecdote cited in [Dropcurb: Junk Removal Estimate](https://dropcurb.com/blog/junk-removal-estimate) (via search summary)
- Software: QuoteIQ markets AI that generates a full estimate from **a single photo** of the pile. — [YouTube Short: Best AI Estimating Software for Junk Removal in 2026](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/KnkWM0uyG_M) (vendor marketing; accuracy unverified)

### Inferences
- Practitioners implicitly use a **"range remotely, lock on site"** protocol. A photo-quote tool should therefore output a **range (low/likely/high tier)** with explicit assumptions: visible volume only, no hidden rooms, light household density, ground-level access. It should also prompt for the information most often missed:
  - wide shots showing pile height
  - other rooms, closets and attic
  - heavy materials (concrete, dirt, tile, drywall, shingles)
  - stairs, floors and elevator
  - carry distance and parking
  - special-disposal items (mattresses, refrigerant appliances, tires, e-waste, paint)
- Systematic underestimation is the expected bias (hidden items and customer framing). Practitioner hedges are to round up on large jobs and keep the minimum firm even when the load comes in smaller.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how far photo quotes miss (e.g., % of jobs adjusted up) from operators. Reddit and YouTube transcripts, where such anecdotes would live, were inaccessible.
- No operator-published "standard question script" was retrievable. The checklist above is assembled from blogs and guides.

## 6. Typical profit margins / gross margin per job, and common pricing mistakes that kill profit

### Takeaway
Practitioner numbers cluster at a **~30% target job margin** (JRA). JRA's model is about **41% direct cost** on a ~$350 ticket, leaving about $206 gross profit, which falls to about **$119–$154 per job** after customer acquisition. Owners who scale report **net margins falling to ~18–20%** (Casey Walsh at about $4MM/yr; Kyle Landwehr's gross margin fell from ~50% in year 1 to ~20% by year 3 after adding management). The most-cited profit killers are:
- underpricing to look competitive
- linear load pricing
- counting only the dump fee
- ignoring weight
- minimums that don't cover the trip
- copying competitors' prices
- per-item menus
- not raising prices as tipping fees rise
- not getting the price in writing

### Cited Findings
**Margins and per-job economics**
- JRA's model: **average job $350**; **average expense per job $143.50** (41% of income, 2-person crew labor including drive, load and dump time, plus disposal and other direct costs); **gross profit about $206.50**. With customer-acquisition cost, total expense is about **66% in year 1 (profit ~$119/job, 34%)**, falling to **56% by year 3 (~$154/job)**. — [JRA: How Much Money are You Making on Each Junk Removal Job?](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-much-money-are-you-making-on-each-junk-removal-job/) (via search excerpt; ~4.8 years old; full line items not visible)
- Casey Walsh (Stand Up Guys Junk Removal) on The Sweaty Startup #105 (2019): about **$4MM/yr** at an **18% profit margin**, 54 employees, 7 locations in 6 states. — [Sweaty Startup blog: How to start a junk removal company with Casey Walsh](https://www.sweatystartup.com/blog/how-to-start-a-junk-removal-company-with-casey-walsh); [Spotify: Episode #105](https://open.spotify.com/episode/1zg9JFf2BHyEW0pT9sIuqA) (via search summary; pre-2022)

**Pricing mistakes practitioners call out**
- **Underpricing to look competitive:** "Business owners want to be competitive, so they price themselves much lower than the local franchises." — [JRA: Two of the Biggest Pricing Mistakes](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/)
- **Linear load pricing** (dividing the full price by 4 or 2), which under-recovers the fixed show-up cost on small loads. — [JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/)
- **Counting only the dump fee:** "Most junk removal operators underprice because they only count the dump fee and forget everything else." Real cost is labor, disposal, vehicle and equipment, and overhead. — vendor guides [GreenRoute](https://greenrouteapp.com/blog/price-junk-removal-services) / [DoorstepHQ](https://doorstephq.com/blog/how-to-estimate-junk-removal-jobs-without-losing-money) (via search summary). One guide says a "competitive" $250 half-load "is barely paying them for the work" once everything is counted. — (same, via search summary)
- **Ignoring weight and dense materials:** concrete, asphalt, dirt and roofing are charged by weight at many disposal sites. A load that fills ¼ of a truck may weigh 2,000 lb. — [Wexford](https://www.wexfordins.com/post/how-to-price-junk-removal); [Haul of the Wild](https://haulofthewildservices.com/pricing/) (heavy debris quoted separately)
- **Minimums that don't cover dispatch plus the dump run.** — [JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/how-to-set-your-junk-removal-pricing/); [Swivl](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-prices)
- **Copying a competitor's price:** "Copying his price copies his margin — which might be terrible." — vendor guide via search summary; JRA likewise says rivals' prices shouldn't be the only input. — [JRA](https://junkremovalauthority.com/two-of-the-biggest-pricing-mistakes-we-see-junk-removal-businesses-make/)
- **Item pricing invites haggling.** Reserve per-item add-ons for special disposal items. — [Swivl](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-prices) (vendor)
- **Stale rates and loose paperwork:** holding prices flat while tipping fees rise is "a slow-motion pay cut," and not confirming the price in writing turns a disputed quote into a discount. — [College Hunks Franchise](https://collegehunksfranchise.com/junk-removal-pricing-sheet/) (franchisor)
- **Missing access and heavy items when undercutting:** Swivl's illustrative example compares a flat **$425** written quote that cleared about **$245** with a **$199** undercut that missed the heavy items and stairs and cleared about **$30**. — [Swivl](https://swivl.tech/blog/junk-removal-prices) (illustrative, vendor)
- **Ignoring dump-site time:** cheap dump fees can cost more in crew time. — [Strong Back Nation episode notes](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/263-strong-back-nation-267486974/episode/smart-strategies-for-junk-removal-efficiency-269028380/)

**Disposal cost reference points mentioned by practitioners and forums**
- LawnSite posters report dump fees of **$18/ton** (county), **$36.50**, **$65/ton** and **$80/ton** (Maryland), or flat **$20–$25 per load** ($50 for trailer plus truck bed). One notes a recycler that takes brick, stone and concrete free. Many posts are old. — [LawnSite: Dump fee!](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/dump-fee.520712/); [LawnSite: What do you charge for disposal?](https://www.lawnsite.com/threads/what-do-you-charge-for-disposal.516545/) (via search summary)
- One guide writer reports about **$120/ton** to dump in Los Angeles. — vendor guide via search summary. Typical landfill rates are about **$50–$100/ton** for household waste. — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/junk-removal-pricing/)

### Inferences
- JRA's 41% direct-cost / ~30% net-after-marketing structure is the best practitioner-grounded per-job template. For a HaulCalc tool, direct cost per job = loaded crew-hours (including drive and dump) + disposal (by weight or by load) + truck/fuel allocation. Price is then set so that direct cost is about 40–50% of price, leaving room for marketing (often 10–20% of revenue for growing operators, per JRA's year-1 vs year-3 gap) and net profit.
- Margins compress as operators scale and add management (Landwehr, Walsh). Solo owner-operators report higher per-job margins partly because their own labor is not priced in, which JRA explicitly warns against.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 operator-published per-job P&L with line items was retrievable. JRA's model is the only detailed one, and it is about 5 years old.
- Could not verify the r/sweatystartup profit anecdote or read Reddit pricing-mistake threads.
