## 1. How landfills and transfer stations charge haulers, and typical 2024–2026 tipping fees (national and regional)

### Takeaway
Most US facilities charge **per ton on a scale**, with a **flat minimum per visit**. Per-cubic-yard, per-load, or per-vehicle pricing shows up mainly at small rural or New England town stations, for pickups and hand-unload trailers, or when the scale is down. EREF's national average **landfill** MSW tipping fee was **$62.28/ton for 2024** and **$63.48/ton in the 2025 report**. Regional averages run from roughly **$43–48/ton in the Southeast/South Central** to **$73–85/ton in the Pacific and Northeast**. However, the **metro transfer stations** that junk haulers actually use often charge **$100–$245/ton** on the West Coast and in the Northeast, which is 2–4x the EREF landfill averages.

### Inferences
- **Fee structure to model:** `disposal = max(min_fee, rate_per_ton × tons) + per-transaction fee + per-item surcharges + penalties (uncovered/unsecured load, wet mattress, mixed/unsorted)`. The fallback when there is no scale (or a per-yard facility) is `rate_per_yd × measured yd³`, typically with a 1–2 yd minimum. Some facilities apply a **1-ton or 2-ton minimum billing weight** to commercial and roll-off loads (Huntington Beach, Texas Disposal Systems, Sarpy County), which strongly penalizes small loads.
- **Per-ton is the dominant model** for commercial vehicles at scaled facilities. Flat per-load or per-vehicle pricing is mostly limited to residential pickups and hand-unload trailers, and is sometimes closed to commercial or decaled vehicles.
- **EREF averages understate what junk haulers pay in metro areas.** EREF measures landfill posted rates, while haulers in cities mostly use transfer stations that add transport, system and host fees. Suggested app defaults (user-overridable):
  - South Central/Southeast: about $45–$65/ton
  - Midwest/Mountain: about $40–$70/ton
  - National default: about $63/ton
  - Northeast metro: about $100–$180/ton
  - Pacific Northwest and California metro: about $120–$245/ton
- The app should let users enter their **actual account rate**, because commercial and contract accounts can be **25–50% below the posted gate rate**.
- Expect **annual increases of about 2–10%**. Many schedules reset on Jan 1, July 1 or Oct 1, so the app should timestamp rates and prompt for updates.

### Gaps
- The full EREF 2025 regional table could not be accessed (erefdn.org was blocked), so 2025 regional dollar values are missing. The EREF C&D national average could not be confirmed either.
- No systematic dataset of **transfer station** (as opposed to landfill) fees exists. The regional transfer-station ranges above are built from individual schedules.
- No Reddit (r/junkremoval, r/sweatystartup) threads surfaced in search, so operator anecdotes are limited to forum posts and operator blogs.

## 2. Per-visit minimums, scale fees, pickups/trailers vs roll-offs, and separate rates for C&D, yard waste, clean fill and shingles

### Takeaway
Minimum charges are near-universal and usually **$10–$47 per visit**, covering roughly the first **240–1,000 lb**. Commercial and roll-off loads sometimes carry **1–2-ton minimum billing weights** or **$150–$375 minimums**. **C&D** is often priced equal to or slightly above MSW in landfills, but it can be **much higher** at Northeast transfer stations (e.g., $200–$300/ton) and much lower at C&D-only facilities in the South (~$35–$66/ton). **Clean, source-separated loads** of yard waste, concrete, brick, dirt and shingles are usually **discounted**, often by 25–75%, and **mixed or unsorted loads are penalized**.

### Inferences
- The app should carry a **material-stream multiplier** relative to the user's MSW rate:
  - yard waste ≈ 0.25–0.75x
  - clean concrete/brick/asphalt ≈ 0.4–0.8x (but see Howard, MD, where dirt costs more than C&D)
  - shingles ≈ 0.6–1.0x
  - C&D ≈ 0.9–1.3x at landfills, but up to 2x at some Northeast transfer stations
- Heavy materials (concrete, dirt, shingles) dominate cost per load **by weight**, even at a discounted per-ton rate.
- A separate **per-visit cost floor** matters for small jobs. With a $20–$47 minimum, a single-item job has a disposal cost of at least the minimum unless it is combined with other jobs in the same dump trip. The app should model disposal **per dump trip**, then allocate it across the jobs on that trip.
- Roll-off and dump-truck loads can be **reclassified as C&D** (Palm Beach) or carry 1–2-ton minimum billing weights, which matters for operators using large dump trucks or roll-offs.

### Gaps
- No national dataset on minimum fees by vehicle type. The ranges above come from individual schedules.
- Scale-house "environmental", "host community" or state surcharges are rarely itemized in excerpts. The NJ REA tax ($3/ton, included in the MCMUA rate) and the forum receipt (franchise fee plus landfill surcharge) are the only explicit examples found.

## 3. Special-item disposal costs to the operator (mattresses, tires, refrigerant appliances, e-waste, paint, propane, batteries, hot tubs, pianos)

### Takeaway
Special items are the biggest variance driver in disposal cost.
- **Mattresses:** about $0 at Mattress Recycling Council (Bye Bye Mattress) sites in CA, CT, RI and OR, subject to per-site caps. Elsewhere $12–$50 each, with penalties for wet or non-recyclable units.
- **Refrigerant appliances:** about $10–$50 each at landfills.
- **Tires:** about $3–$25 each, or $212–$500/ton for commercial loads.
- **CRT TVs:** about $20–$75 each. Flat screens are $0–$30.
- **Paint:** free through PaintCare drop-off sites, subject to volume limits.

- **Hot tubs and pianos** cost little in tipping fees (tens of dollars). Their cost is labor and demolition.

## 4. How much a full load weighs (15–20 yd³ dump trailer or junk truck), and lb/yd³ by material

### Takeaway
Mixed household junk is light: about **150–250 lb/yd³** (range 100–400), so a full 15-yd load of household junk weighs about **1.1–1.9 tons** (one operator cites **~2,300 lb** for a 15-yd trailer). Mixed C&D is about **420–500 lb/yd³**, brush/yard debris about **130 lb/yd³**, and broken concrete, brick and dirt about **1,800–2,400 lb/yd³**. Heavy materials hit the truck's legal payload long before the box is full.

### Cited Findings
- 15-yard trailer of household trash: about **2,300 lb**. 15-yard of old deck boards: about **4,800 lb**. 15-yard packed with sheetrock: up to **7,500 lb**. ### Inferences
- **Suggested density defaults for the app (lb/yd³, loose as loaded):**

| Material | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed household junk / furniture | 200 | 100–400 |
| Mattresses / upholstered | ~100–150 (weak evidence) | — |
| Mixed C&D / remodel debris | 450 | 300–500 |
| Yard waste / brush | 130 | 100–250 (leaves/grass not sourced) |
| Shingles | not sourced (gap) | — |
| Concrete / brick / dirt (broken, loose) | 2,000 | 1,800–2,400 |

- **Full-load disposal cost examples** (15-yd household load at about 2,300 lb, 1.15 tons):
  - ~$52 at $45/ton (South)
  - ~$72 at $63/ton (national EREF)
  - ~$130 at $113/ton (NJ)
  - ~$190 at $165/ton (Seattle)
  - ~$280 at $243/ton (King County)
  - The same box filled with C&D (about 3.4 tons at 450 lb/yd³) costs about 3x as much.
- Concrete and dirt loads are **payload-limited, not volume-limited**. A 15-yd box of broken concrete would be about 15 tons, far beyond typical junk trucks and dump trailers, so the app should cap heavy-material volume by the vehicle's payload rating and price by weight.
- Weight estimation error is the biggest disposal-modeling risk. The app should let operators **calibrate density from their own scale tickets** (tons ÷ estimated yd³ per job type).

### Gaps
- No measured, junk-removal-specific dataset of average tons per truckload was found. The 2,300-lb figure is a single operator's estimate.
- Shingle density, leaves and grass density, and the EPA 2016 furniture/bulky-item factors were not retrieved.
- No data on typical payload ratings of common junk truck builds (e.g., 1-800-GOT-JUNK-style 15-yd box trucks) was gathered in this pass.

## 5. Offsets: scrap metal value, donations, and recycling rebates

### Takeaway
Scrap steel offsets are real but small and volatile. Light iron and appliances fetched about **$110–$215 per ton** (roughly **4–11¢/lb**) in Sept–Oct 2026, so a ~150–270-lb appliance is worth about **$7–$30**. That roughly cancels its weight-based tip fee but not a refrigerant fee. Donation avoids tipping fees only for resaleable items, and charities (ReStore, etc.) accept selectively. Credible estimates suggest only **~10–33%** of collected material is actually donated, against marketing claims of about 60% diversion. Program "rebates" (CA e-waste, MRC mattresses) flow mainly to recyclers. For haulers, the benefit is **free drop-off rather than cash**.

- Pennsylvania yards (Oct 2, 2026): appliances **$112.50/GT**, light iron $120/GT. ### Inferences
- Model scrap as a **per-item or per-lb credit**: default ~5¢/lb for light iron (range 3–11¢), with an editable price and date stamp. Given the ~33% swings over 45 days, the app should not hard-code scrap prices.
- Per-appliance net economics (illustrative):
  - Washer at 150 lb: scrap $7–$15 vs tip-fee avoided at $63/ton ≈ $5, so a net positive of about $12–$20 if the operator is already passing a scrap yard.
  - Refrigerator at 270 lb: scrap $13–$27, minus a $15–$50 freon fee, nets about −$37 to +$12.
  - Scrap rarely justifies a dedicated trip.
- Donation offsets: assume **10–30% of a mixed household load by volume** is donatable in good-condition cleanouts, and near 0% for trashed or wet loads. The savings equal avoided tip fees, minus the extra stop time and any charity pickup fees. In cheap-landfill regions ($45/ton), the per-ton tip savings (~$45) can be smaller than the labor cost of an extra stop, so donation is mainly a marketing and ESG benefit there. In $150–$245/ton markets it is a material cost lever.
- The largest practical offsets come from **routing special items to free program channels** (mattresses in MRC states, PaintCare, CA e-waste, free flat-screen recyclers) rather than cash rebates.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the actual dollar savings from donation for a junk removal operator, or measured donatable share by job type.
- Non-ferrous scrap values for junk-removal-relevant items (aluminum patio furniture, copper or brass, motors, catalytic converters, lead-acid batteries) were not gathered.
- No evidence on whether ReStores accept bulk drop-offs from junk haulers versus homeowner donations only. Policies are affiliate-specific.

## 6. Dump trips per day and time spent at the dump

### Takeaway
No measured industry dataset exists. Facility pages show queues from **minutes to 1–2 hours at peak (especially Saturdays)**, unload windows as short as **15 minutes**, and **afternoon cutoffs** (often 3:45–4:30 pm weekdays, about 2 pm Saturdays) that cap the number of daily trips. Operator and vendor content implies **2–3 jobs per truck per day** for small operators, with multi-stop loads (landfill, scrap, donation, mattress recycler) able to consume a whole day.

### Cited Findings
- Leavenworth County, KS transfer station: even after a 2022 outbound scale upgrade, **1–2-hour waits** are "normal" at times, especially Saturdays. When crowded, customers get **15 minutes to unload** once backed into a bay. Weekday trucks must be in line by **3:45 pm** and off the scale by 4:00 pm; Saturday cutoff is 2:00 pm. Queues form when special-handling loads tie up operators. — [Leavenworth County FAQ](https://www.leavenworthcounty.gov/departments/transfer_station/frequently_asked_questions/index.php) [OFFICIAL]
- Indianapolis: waits of **up to an hour** at the Saturday-only city station; the South Side Landfill has significantly shorter waits and is open Mon–Sat. — [Veteran Hauling](https://veteranhauling.com/blog/where-to-dump-junk-trash-indianapolis-indiana-every-dump-landfill-transfer-station/) [OPERATOR]
- Snohomish County, WA Southwest station: open 7 days, 7 am–4:30 pm, and customers unload their own vehicles. — [Snohomish County](https://snohomishcountywa.gov/465/Facility-Locations) [OFFICIAL]
- Trip caps: Morgan County, GA limits C&D trailers of 10 yd or less to **2 trips/day**. Honolulu caps residents at 2 loads/day, and commercial users must call ahead. — [Morgan County](https://www.morgancountyga.gov/149/Solid-Waste-Sanitation); [Honolulu](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/waste-drop-off-locations/) [OFFICIAL]
- EPA transfer station manual: municipal packer trucks unload "generally once or twice per day", and a station can halt intake when no empty transfer trailer is positioned. This is a collection-truck analogy only. — [EPA Waste Transfer Stations manual](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-03/documents/r02002.pdf) [OFFICIAL, older]
- Loading time ranges from **15 minutes for a single item to 2+ hours for a packed garage**. Mixed loads needing several facility stops "eat up an entire day". — [Pinky's Junk Removal](https://pinkysjunkremoval.com/how-same-day-junk-removal-works/); [Dropcurb Broken Arrow](https://dropcurb.com/ok/broken-arrow/bulk-junk-removal) [OPERATOR/VENDOR]
- A solo operator typically completes **2–3 jobs per day**, 5 days a week. — [Dropcurb "Is junk removal a good business"](https://dropcurb.com/blog/is-junk-removal-a-good-business) [VENDOR]
- A forum dump-trailer owner reports $15–$20 per trip at a per-ton landfill, implying light trailer loads of roughly 0.25–0.5 ton (undated). — [TractorByNet](https://www.tractorbynet.com/forums/threads/dumpster-cleanup.244309/) [OPERATOR anecdote]

### Inferences
- Model dump-trip time as `drive_to_facility + queue (default 15–20 min weekday, 45–60 min Saturday) + scale-in/out (~5 min) + unload (~10–20 min with a dump body; ~20–45 min hand-unload of a box truck)`, plus `return drive`, plus **~15–30 min per extra stop** for scrap, donation or mattress recycling. The unload times are estimates, as no source measured them.
- Daily trip capacity is bounded by facility hours and cutoffs (often ~3:45–4:30 pm). Operators doing 2–3 jobs per day with partial loads may **consolidate jobs into 1–2 dump runs per day**. This affects how per-visit minimums should be allocated across jobs.
- Hand-unload vs dump body is a meaningful labor-time variable. Some facilities price hand-unload vehicles differently (Salt Lake flat $16) and require self-unloading (Snohomish).

### Gaps
- No measured junk-removal-specific data on dump trips per day, average queue time, or unload time. No Reddit or YouTube operator data was retrievable in this session (search did not surface r/junkremoval threads, and page fetch was blocked).
- No data on how operators allocate a single dump fee across multiple jobs on the same run.
